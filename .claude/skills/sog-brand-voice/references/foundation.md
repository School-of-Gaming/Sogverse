# Foundation: who we are, and what we call ourselves

## Purpose

Children build their identities and their friendships in two places now, and only one of
them has adults who know what they are doing. School of Gaming puts qualified adults in the
other one.

We exist so that every child, wherever they live and whatever they can afford, can enter a
safe, moderated, imaginative world where the skills that decide their adult life are
learned by playing rather than by being told.

**One discipline runs through everything.** School of Gaming has a lot of vocabulary, and it
would be easy to turn every sentence into a parade of proprietary terms. Do not. A term
earns its place when it makes a thing clearer or truer, not because it is ours. When two
terms would do, use the one the reader already knows. And one rule sits above the rest:
**Sogverse is a gift we give, not a test we set.** Nobody outside our walls owes us fluency
in our own vocabulary.

## Values, in priority order

When two pull against each other, the higher one wins.

1. **Safety.** Structural, not aspirational. We do not promise safety, we build it, and then
   we point at the build. Safety outranks everything, including a good story and a smooth
   sale.
2. **Belonging.** Real friendships, not just gaming buddies. No child is left without a
   friend. The gamer's oath, the small groups and the community all exist so that a child
   has somewhere they are known.
3. **Growth.** Learning that looks like playing. The skills that decide an adult life,
   absorbed through well guided play rather than instruction.

If a piece of copy serves none of the three, ask why it exists.

## Positioning

**School of Gaming is the game of games: a story world called Sogverse where children learn
to be good digital citizens by playing, guided by professional Game Educators, across a
school year built like a television season.**

Four things in that sentence must survive into any piece of copy that describes us:

| Element | Why it cannot be dropped |
| :-- | :-- |
| A story world, not a venue | Our world grows whenever a new gamer arrives with an idea; a building has a ceiling |
| Learning by playing | Our pedagogical claim. Play is the mechanism, not the sweetener |
| Professional Game Educators | What separates us from an unsupervised server. The single most reassuring fact we own |
| A season, not a course | Why families stay. Nobody leaves a story in the middle |

**Games are dimensions of Sogverse, never the definition of the offer.** Name several
titles or none: "clubs in Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite and more", or simply "clubs".
"Minecraft clubs" tells a family the product is one game, and that is learned in one
sentence and unlearned only by contradicting ourselves.

## What School of Gaming is not

- **Not a gaming camp with a worksheet stapled to it.** The play is the pedagogy. Copy that
  sounds like it is apologising for the gaming gets rewritten.
- **Not a coding bootcamp.** Technology is one of four Yty-Elements (Wit). Most of the
  Human Skills we build have nothing to do with a keyboard.
- **Not a screen time solution.** We never position ourselves against screens. We are not
  the good screen time; we are game education.
- **Not a metaverse.** The word is ours only as a joke about the industry, once per piece
  (`vocabulary.md`).
- **Not neutral about behaviour.** Kindness, honesty and courage are the physics of our
  world, not style choices. Copy that is fun but morally weightless is off brand.

## The vision statement and the slogans

**The vision statement is "Where Screen Time Becomes Quality Time."**, in Finnish
**"Ruutuajasta laatuaikaa."**: those words, that capitalisation, that full stop, wherever
prose states it. It meets a parent's existing worry and offers a trade rather than an
argument. The home hero and the social card draw it line broken and without the full stop;
that is a display treatment of the same sentence, the only one, and no licence to
re-punctuate it anywhere else. At most once per page; never replaced, never run beside a
second line of equal weight.

**Five slogans exist.** Each appears at most once per page, never two in one piece, with
capitalisation and full stops exactly as written and never an exclamation mark. A reader
who meets two concludes we have not decided.

| Slogan | Its job | Where it is placed |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **"Learning by Gaming."** (fi "Oppimista pelaten.") | States the method | The home page's how it works section |
| **"Scouts of the Online Age"** | Institutional credibility, for municipalities, schools, press, partners, Gedu recruitment | Gedu registration |
| **"Children first. Always."** | The ethical spine: safety, safeguarding, hard decisions. It settles arguments; use it sparingly in public so it keeps its weight | The anti-bullying page |
| **"Ambassadors of Positive Gaming."** | Names the role our people play | Deliberately unplaced |
| **"By playing, a better world and better people."** | The mission, for funders and anything about why we exist | Deliberately unplaced |

Placing an unplaced slogan is a decision to raise with the owner, not a gap to fill. The
vision statement may share a page with one slogan, lower and smaller, never with two.

**"Children first. Always." outranks everything in a conflict.** If copy is charming and
slightly at odds with that line, the copy is wrong.

## The two anchors for explaining us

**For a parent asking what this is, the Hogwarts parallel:** "School of Gaming is to digital
citizenship roughly what Hogwarts is to wizardry. Our gamers are the students, our Gedus are
the professors, and instead of spells they pick up skills that will matter for the rest of
their lives." Use it with new parents, partners and press; never inside Sogverse, in gamer
copy or in lesson materials, where we compare ourselves to nothing.

**For anyone asking whether to trust us, the Scouts:** "We are the Scouts of the Online
Age." A values led movement with trained, accountable adult leaders, badges earned by doing,
a community, and skills for the terrain children actually live in, which is online. Never
imply affiliation with any scouting organisation; we draw a parallel about how a movement
works.

In a long partner pitch, both, in that order: Hogwarts opens the door, Scouts is what is
inside the room.

## The loop: gamers become Gedus

A child joins at seven, grows up inside Sogverse, and when they age out the path turns: they
can train as a Gedu and come back as the adult guiding the next seven year old. To parents
it is a hobby with a horizon; to gamers, Gedus are what a gamer can become; to partners, a
skills pipeline that staffs itself.

**Write the loop as a fact, never as a promise, and only as far as it is true.** "Gamers can
become Gedus" describes an open path. "Your child could have a career with us one day" is a
sales line about someone else's child. Before writing that former gamers already work as
Gedus, ask whether it is true yet.

## The names

### School of Gaming and Sogverse

**School of Gaming is the brand; Sogverse is the platform.** School of Gaming is who we are:
the company, the identity, the name a parent was told by a school or another parent, and the
one that must survive a crowded inbox or a browser tab. Sogverse is the thing that does the
work: the app families log in to, and the world gamers play in.

**Sogverse is one thing.** The gaming universe and the software are the same object; never
describe it as having two meanings. Do not write "the Sogverse platform", "Sogverse (our
online environment)" or "Sogverse, our virtual world and app". The verb tells the reader
which face they are looking at: *log in to Sogverse*, *your Sogverse account* is practical;
*Sogverse is still being mapped* is the world.

**The two names are a progression, the same way Game Educator → Gedu is.** A stranger is
told "School of Gaming" first, because it is the only name that means anything to them yet.
The product teaches them "Sogverse": on the page they log into, in the mail that welcomes
them. So Sogverse is never handed to a stranger cold. It may appear on a public surface only
where the copy introduces it, and the Level 1 gloss is **"Sogverse, the private online world
where our clubs happen"**, which describes a world and a piece of software at once.

**Which name a sentence takes follows what the sentence is about, not who reads it.** Copy
about us says School of Gaming even inside the product (a promise about safety, who invoices
the municipality, who holds the copyright). Copy about the thing says Sogverse wherever the
reader has met it (what a family logs into, what stores an account).

**No article.** Write Sogverse, never "the Sogverse": with the article it sounds like a
category in the family of "the metaverse". In "the Sogverse shop" or "the Sogverse team" the
article belongs to *shop* and *team*, and that is correct. Finnish and the other languages
follow their own grammar.

**The account possessive is with the brand.** "Your School of Gaming account", "your School
of Gaming password", "your School of Gaming parent PIN": the account is a relationship with
the company, and Sogverse is the door it opens. The company is also who acts on an account
(we received the request, we will send the link).

**The lockup is `School of Gaming – Sogverse`**: brand first, platform second, separated by
a spaced en dash (U+2013), never a hyphen or an em dash. The dash is part of the mark (Mikko,
2 Nov 2026). Use it wherever a string carries both names; the email header and the site's
page title are the canonical uses. Where room runs out, the brand goes alone: the email
sender name is "School of Gaming", because an inbox truncates hard and "School of Gaming –
Sogv…" is worse than the short name. An em dash between the names ("Sogverse — School of
Gaming — runs clubs") is a different construct that claims the two are the same thing;
state the relationship instead: Sogverse is School of Gaming's platform.

### Gedu, and the role

**Gedu is a product name: capital G in every language, never translated, never used cold.**
It carries the signed in product, where the reader knows it, and appears in public prose
only with its gloss ("Gedus, our trained Game Educators"; "Gedut eli koulutetut
pelikasvattajamme"). One public surface is carved out: the Gedu registration page, whose
title may use the word cold, because nobody arrives there without having been told what a
Gedu is. Nothing adjacent to that page shares the carve out.

Wherever Gedu is not the word, each language has exactly one word for the role, chosen by
native speakers on 13 Aug 2026: **Game Educator** (en, capitalised, a defined term),
**pelikasvattaja** (fi), **spelfostrare** (sv), **animateur / animatrice** (fr). One word
covers marketing, product and legal text alike. The two lawyer authored Roblox programme
documents are the exception (`vocabulary.md`).

### The Princi-Pal and Reksi

The Princi-Pal is Mikko Perälä, CEO of School of Gaming: one man, who runs the school from
the northeast tower and also signs the compliance documents. Copy never implies the
character and the executive are different people. He is **the Princi-Pal** in English,
Swedish and French (the hyphen and the second capital are the joke, and the joke is that he
is on your side) and **Reksi** in Finnish (Mikko, 2 Nov 2026). Never "Principal",
"Principal Gamer" or "pääpelaaja". How he speaks is `reksi.md`.

### The legal name and the trademarks

The company is **School of Gaming Galactic Oy**; School of Gaming and SOG are the names it
trades under. Brand copy never uses the legal name. Legal, contractual, invoicing and formal
partner copy (terms, the privacy policy, contracts, funding applications) uses it at least
once, and Mikko's accountable signature may carry it in genuinely formal documents.

The logo is a registered EU trade mark, so **® goes with the logo**, at its first or most
prominent appearance, once per page or screen. Running text that writes "School of Gaming"
as words needs no symbol. Never claim ® outside the EU where the mark may not be registered,
and never on anything unregistered: an unregistered sub-brand takes ™, after checking.

**Gedu is planned as a registered word mark** (applied for from autumn 2026). Until it is
registered, put neither ® nor ™ on it without checking with our trademark representative,
since a premature symbol can complicate the filing. Consistent use strengthens the
application: always "a Gedu" or "our Gedus", capital G, a proper role; never lowercase as
though it were an ordinary job title. Every correct use of Gedu is also trademark hygiene,
which is part of why teacher, tutor and coach are banned as substitutes.

### Gedu Academy and Tellus

**Gedu Academy**, both capitals, no article ("at Gedu Academy"): where Game Educators are
trained, at geduacademy.com, in its own adult, professional voice. **Tellus**, capital T, no
article: our word for the physical world, at Levels 2 and 3 only; at Levels 0 and 1 write
"everyday life" or "at home".

## The world

### Yty and the Four Yty-Elements

Yty is the force that keeps Sogverse in balance, built from four elements: our model of a
child's four relationships, and how growth is made concrete, badged and measured.

| Element | The relationship | What it covers |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Harmony** | With yourself | Balance, emotional control, self acceptance, rest, knowing when to stop |
| **Glow** | With others | Empathy, kindness, belonging, friendship, communication |
| **Valor** | With society | Teamwork, innovation, civic courage, trying the hard thing |
| **Wit** | With technology | Critical thinking, media literacy, curiosity, finding your way in the digital world |

Valor is the relationship with society and Wit with technology; those are the pair that gets
swapped. Never reorder the four, never invent a fifth. The element definitions live in two
places, the `yty` messages namespace and the Yty constants module, and a change to either is
unfinished until the other matches. Their colours are SOG-UI's.

For parents and partners the elements map onto Human Skills, the vocabulary that audience
already owns (`vocabulary.md`).

### Places

Sogverse is mapped. Writers working in the world can draw on named locations, each with its
own character: **Frostcrossing** (Viimataipale), **Emerald Bay** (Smaragdilahti),
**Spelheim**, **Sogwood**, **Nautilus**, **Oddnook**, **Torchton**, among others still being
charted. A place with a Finnish and an English name keeps one canonical pair.

On Tellus, name a physical place only where something actually happens there now: the
studio in Lauttasaari, Helsinki, where in person clubs meet, is fine in a photo caption.
Do not build copy around a headquarters: we will be adding languages and places, and the
office may move (Mikko, 2 Nov 2026). The company's legal address belongs in terms, invoicing
and formal partner copy only.
