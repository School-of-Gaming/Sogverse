# Hard moments, and saying what keeps a child safe

This is where gamified brands break. The failure always has the same shape: the fiction,
built to create delight, is reached for as a shield when something has gone wrong. Parents
can smell it, and once they have, they distrust everything charming we ever said.

**Sogverse is a place we invite people into. It is never a place we hide in.**

The facts inside the examples below (times, names, amounts) are illustrative. A real message
uses the real ones, and states nothing the product or the people involved cannot make true.

## The screen time anxious parent

**Level 1. Never argue, never cite studies at them, never imply the worry is silly.**

The sequence: acknowledge the worry as reasonable, say the specific thing we already do about
it, name what they can watch for, and offer a way to be involved. The offer is what turns
anxiety into partnership.

> Thank you for writing, and for being honest about it. You are not the first parent to ask,
> and it is a fair question.
>
> Here is what we do on our side. [The structural thing, true of this club: a break in the
> lesson, how long it runs, what the Gedu watches for.]
>
> On your side, the most useful thing is to ask Anni what her group is working on rather than
> how long she has been on. You will get a much better read on how it is going than a clock
> will give you.

What is absent: defensiveness, data, reassurance that gaming is fine actually. One glossed
term at most, one flat fact, one action for the parent.

## A behavioural incident

Two cases, and conflating them is a serious error.

**In-world mischief, low stakes** (rude in chat, broke a build, dominated a group). Level 3
with the gamer, Level 1 with the parent if the parent is involved at all. With the gamer the
Toxie frame is a gift: it separates the child from the conduct and gives a way back without
humiliation.

> That was a Toxie move, and I do not think it was you. Go and say something decent to Ville,
> then come back and we will finish the build together.

**Real harm** (bullying, harassment, grooming risk, threats, anything the safeguarding policy
covers). **Level 0, no exceptions.** The Toxie frame is for teaching, never for reporting.
"A Toxie targeted your son" to a parent whose son was harassed is the worst sentence this
brand could produce.

> I am writing about something that happened in the lesson on Tuesday 14 July at about 5.15pm.
>
> Another participant sent Leo repeated messages that were personal and hostile. Our Gedu,
> Sanna, saw it within minutes, removed the participant from the room, and stayed with Leo for
> the rest of the lesson. Leo was calm when it ended and told Sanna he wanted to come back on
> Thursday.
>
> The other participant's access is suspended pending review, and we have kept a full record
> of the messages. If you would like to see it, say so and I will send it.
>
> I would like to speak with you directly. I am reachable today until 6pm and any time
> tomorrow.

Named Gedu, a specific time, what we did in order, what happens next, a direct line to a
human, no lore.

## Billing, refunds and money

**Level 0, always.** No Sogo, no Yty, no whimsy. Amounts plain, dates in full. If we are at
fault, say so in the first sentence without a preamble.

> We charged you twice for June. That was our error.
>
> The duplicate charge of €59 has been refunded and should reach your statement within 5
> business days. Your next payment is on 1 August, for €59.
>
> If it has not appeared by 28 July, reply to this email and I will chase it myself.

No apology inflation ("we sincerely regret any inconvenience"): one admission, one remedy,
one deadline, one way to escalate.

**The 30-day money-back guarantee is a trust asset.** State it plainly and early, in
enrolment copy, on pricing, wherever a hesitant parent needs the risk taken off the table.
Hedging a guarantee destroys the reassurance it exists to give. It may say no more than the
terms and the About FAQ's billing answers promise for every club.

## Safeguarding

**Level 0. No brand voice at all: a procedure.** Write it the way you would want to receive
one about your own child: fast, factual, chronological, not a word chosen for effect.

Every safeguarding message:
- goes out within the time the policy sets, never later, even if the picture is incomplete
- says what happened, when, and who observed it
- names the responsible adult and how to reach them directly
- says what has already been done
- says what happens next, and by when
- contains no lore, no reassurance we cannot back, and no speculation about intent
- never asks the parent to keep anything confidential

A safeguarding message containing a metaphor has failed review.

## Outages and service failures

**Level 0, with one narrow exception.** Families need three facts first: what is broken, what
it means for them today, and when we will speak next.

> The Minecraft server has been down since about 3.30pm today, so the 4pm to 6pm lessons
> cannot run. Tonight's lesson is cancelled and nobody is charged for it. We will post a
> replacement in the schedule tomorrow, and we will update you by 8pm tonight whether or
> not the server is back.

Once the facts are delivered, and only if the failure was brief and harmless, one light
closing line is allowed after the substance, never instead of it, and never when anyone lost
work, money or a lesson they were counting on: "Some of you are already blaming the Toxies.
We are not ruling it out." About once a year.

## Saying what keeps a child safe

**Level 0 or 1, never higher.** No safety claim is improved by the word Yty.

**State the mechanism, never the intention.** Every competitor promises to keep children
safe, and a promise is worth what the institution is worth, which a parent reading at 9pm
cannot know. What we have is design: things that are safe because they are
structurally impossible. "There are no private messages" is checkable; "we take bullying
very seriously" is what everyone says.

**Only mechanisms verified true.** Check a safety sentence against the code and the About
page FAQ before writing it, not against what a feature was meant to do. A mechanism we want
but do not have is a feature request, never a sentence.

**Verified, as stated on the home page and the About FAQ (2 Nov 2026):**
- There are no private messages. Chat in a lesson goes to the whole group, never to one
  child privately, and the Gedu sees it. It is kept, so the club's Gedus and our admins can
  look back at it, which is what makes moderation possible.
- A club's virtual room opens five minutes before the lesson and closes five minutes after.
  Only the group's members, its Gedus and our staff can enter, and the server checks that
  on every join.
- Lessons are not recorded: nothing in the app records, transcribes or stores the voice or
  video, and the room has no recording controls.
- Buying anything needs a four digit parent PIN that only the parent knows, and a child
  cannot get into the parent account without it.
- Every gamer account is created by a parent and linked to their account, and the parent
  chooses how the child signs in.
- A gamer account carries an internal address rather than the child's real email, unless the
  parent chose to give the child a sign-in with their own address. A Gedu's roster and the
  room show the child's first name, age and any game username, never the child's email or
  the family surname.
- We require every Gedu to show a criminal record extract for working with children. That
  is a requirement, worded as one, never as an outcome every Gedu has met: certification
  does not wait for it.
- Every Gedu is interviewed and completes the Gedu training (Mikko's word, 2 Nov 2026).

**Not true of Sogverse; never write these** (they come from the Safety Standard and older
brand material):
- "We hold no real names and no email addresses for any child", or that a gamer is known by
  a Display Name and an Age-Bracket and nothing else. We hold a first name, the birth month
  and year, the family surname, and in some cases the child's own email.
- That a Gedu can message a parent without seeing their contact details. A Gedu's roster
  shows the parent's address.
- That report pictures are only ever in-game screenshots, never photos of children. Nothing
  in the product limits what a Gedu attaches, and in-person clubs meet in rooms, so it is
  not a rule we can state (Mikko, 3 Nov 2026). Say what is true: the pictures are of the
  lesson rather than of one child, and only that group's families see them.

**Advertising, said exactly** (checked against the code, 2 Nov 2026). True: nothing about a
child reaches an advertising platform, and a child's browsing never does: the ad scripts
never load for a signed-in gamer, on any page. Also true, so never write the opposite: we do
use data about parents for advertising (a hashed email and account id, and which product
they signed up for, when they accepted the cookies), and our marketing email profile of a
parent carries each child's birth year and gender, never a name. So "no data collection for
advertising" / "ei datankeräystä mainontaa varten" is not true as a blanket line. Write the
child half, which is the one that matters to a worried parent: "Nothing about your child is
ever shared with an advertising platform."

**"A Gedu is in every lesson" is a promise we make to parents** (the owner, 3 Nov 2026), for
clubs, camps and events alike, and the shortest, strongest safety sentence we own: use it.
It is a staffing commitment rather than something the product enforces (checked against the
code, 2 Nov 2026): a group has Gedus assigned, an absent Gedu's lesson is covered by a
substitute or cancelled, and the admin dashboard flags any group with members and no Gedu.
So write it as the promise it is ("a Gedu is in every lesson"), never as a technical
guarantee ("the system ensures", "automatically").

**Tone rules for safety copy**
- **Never sell fear.** Do not describe what could happen to a child elsewhere to make us look
  better. Parents are already afraid.
- **Never claim perfection.** Not "completely safe", not "nothing can go wrong". Say what is
  structurally prevented and who is watching the rest.
- **Compliance frameworks** (the UK Online Safety Act, GDPR, the UK Children's Code) reassure
  institutions; name them in partner and press copy, not to parents, to whom they read as a
  company talking about itself.
- **Data minimisation is a strength, stated exactly.** We ask for what running a club needs
  and no more (a birth month and year, not the day). Mikko's line, signed: "We keep the data
  minimal so the safety can be maximal." Mikko Perälä, Princi-Pal, School of Gaming.
- **Every gamer is linked to a verified parent, so a conflict can be finished rather than
  merely blocked.** Most end between the gamers themselves with the Gedu as referee; a parent
  is a later step, not a first one. Never present that link as a threat ("we can always tell
  your parents"): it is a net, not surveillance.
