# Surfaces and formatting

`SKILL.md` has the registers table. This file has what each surface demands beyond it.

## The public website and landing pages

**Level 1** on the home page, the shop, product pages, About, enrolment and landing pages;
prices and terms at 0. A parent should understand what this is within eight seconds and know
what to do within thirty.

**The reader is a parent** who loves their child, is not sure gaming is good for them, and has
often been told by other institutions that it is not; many arrive carrying quiet guilt, some
fresh from an argument about it. They want four things, in this order: **their child is safe,
something is being learned, they can stay involved without becoming a gamer, and permission
to stop feeling bad.** Be calm, specific and adult: never breathless, never defensive. Lead
with the concrete (who supervises, what happens in a lesson, what changes in their child),
and only then open the door to the story.

**Structure that works:** a plain language hero, then the concrete facts (who supervises, how
long, what it costs, how to start), then the invitation into the world. Never invert it.
Sogverse is the reward for reading, not the toll for entry.

| Element | Length | Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Hero headline | Up to 12 words | Plain language, no lore terms |
| Hero subhead | Up to 30 words | One sentence a sceptical parent would repeat to their partner |
| Section headings | 3 to 7 words | Sentence case; questions are allowed and often better |
| Section body | 40 to 80 words | Never a wall; break at every idea |
| Primary button | 2 to 4 words | Verb first: "Find a club", "See the schedule". Never "Learn more" |

**Pages are named for what a parent would search**, never "Our offering", "Solutions" or
"Discover". Keep core navigation labels stable: ad hoc renaming is how an information
architecture rots. The agreed parent facing names are Clubs / Kerhot, Camps / Leirit, For
parents / Opas vanhemmille, About / Meistä; the Finnish site also carries Syntymäpäivät.

**Prices vary by club**, so copy never presents one figure as the price of School of Gaming:
write "from €59 per month" or "prices vary by club", and put the real figure on the club
itself. A parent who sees one number in an ad and another at checkout has been misled,
whether or not we meant it.

**A slug, a page title and a description are copy too**, and a stranger meets them cold in a
search result: lead with School of Gaming, never Sogverse.

## Partners, press, schools and municipalities

People assessing whether we are credible, safe, measurable and worth being associated with,
often without ever having played a game and quietly worried about looking foolish. They want
a defensible pedagogical model, a safeguarding story, evidence, and a concept they can explain
to their own board in one sentence. **Level 1; plain, confident, evidence first.** Lead with
Human Skills, the vocabulary they already own, and bring in the Four Yty-Elements second as the
structure underneath. Do not hide the world, but let it arrive as evidence of design rather
than as decoration. This is the one audience where naming our word choices is worth doing
(telling an education officer we do not say "soft skills", and why), and where the Hogwarts
parallel and the Scouts of the Online Age work best (`foundation.md`). Name compliance
frameworks here, not to parents.

## The parent's side of the app

My SOG, enrolment, the schedule, spending controls, the PIN, settings. **Level 1; money and
account at 0.** Plain, calm and specific: a parent here is doing admin, not reading a story.
Say what happened, what happens next, and what they can do.

## The gamer's side

The gamer's My SOG, the virtual room, chat, Yty, Quests. **Level 3, written to the gamer.**
Full canon, no glossing, no winking at the fiction from outside it. Direct address ("you",
"your group", "your Gedu"), present tense, stakes visible. Enthusiasm is earned with
specifics, not adjectives. Never explain a joke; never say "kids" or "children" to their
face.

- **Ages 7 to 10** need shorter sentences, one instruction at a time, and an obvious next
  step. **11 and up** need the opposite of condescension above all, and permission to be a
  little sardonic. When one string serves both, write for the older half and make the
  structure obvious enough for the younger.
- **Moderation is Level 0 even inside a Level 3 room.** An enforcement message is plain,
  names the behaviour, states the consequence, and says how to appeal. Conflicts are settled
  first between the gamers with the Gedu as referee; copy that jumps straight to consequences
  misrepresents how we work.
- **Never invite a gamer to share** a real name, a school, a location or an age in a group
  space. The design keeps that information out of shared spaces; copy must not pull it back
  in.
- **Shared surfaces** (an enrolment card, the "before the first lesson" guide, the gamer's
  copy of a confirmation) are read by a parent and a gamer together. Procedural copy there is
  written once, at the plainer level, in one voice.

## The Gedu's side

My SOG for Gedus, invoicing, substitutions, rosters, reports. **Peer to peer, practical, warm
and specific; Level 0 for anything operational.** Give the reason beside the rule, because a
Gedu adapts on the spot in front of a group and cannot phone anyone. A Gedu reading a
safeguarding step at 8 in the morning should not have to decode a metaphor.

**Aspiring Gedus** (registration, Gedu Academy recruitment) are adults: respect their existing
skill, be direct about the standard, and let the structure be the appeal. Never recruit
adults with the enchantment we use on children; an adult sold a fantasy about a job feels
manipulated. "A career, not a course" is the register.

## Admin

**Level 0.** "Users" is legitimate here: admin copy names a population in the database.

## Email

**Level 1 for transactional and onboarding mail, 2 for story and community, 0 for anything
in `hard-moments.md`.**

- **Subject lines**: 4 to 8 words, specific, no lore term unless the family has been with us
  a long while.
- **Length**: under 100 words transactional, 120 to 250 routine; longer only for Season
  openings and parents' evening material, broken with subheadings.
- **Every parent email holds** one concrete fact with a date or a time, one thing the parent
  can do, and one way to reach a human. Mail that is only atmosphere does not get opened
  twice.
- **A mail written by a person is signed by that person**, never "The School of Gaming
  Team". Season and values mail may be signed by Reksi / the Princi-Pal.

## The weekly Gedu message (the lesson report)

Written by the club's Gedu to the families of the group: service tone, friendly, never
selling. What the group did, what it practised, one tip or one thing to notice at home.
Write the way a good teacher writes a note home: specific, short, no lore beyond a glossed
Yty-Element. It ends with a Library article or a free community event, never prices, codes
or trials. For a municipality club's families it is the only message they get from us,
signed by the club's Gedu, and nothing anywhere markets to them.

## The store

**Level 3**, inside the world. A name of 2 to 5 words, a description of 25 to 50: what it is,
what it means, price last. Prices in Sogo are a plain number and the word ("240 Sogo"),
never pluralised. Wherever real money appears it is in euros, in plain language, clearly
apart from Sogo so no child can confuse the two. Until Sogo has a defined rate, say what it
buys but never how fast it is earned ("just three lessons away"). Never invent scarcity and
never put countdown pressure on children.

## Formatting

| Rule | Right | Wrong |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Single durations | 90 minutes · 3 hours | 90min · 90 mins · 1.5h |
| Combined durations | 2h 45m | 2 hours 45 minutes · 2:45 |
| Currency, English | €59 · €59 per month · from €59 | 59€ · 59 EUR · €59.00 |
| Currency, Finnish | 59 € · alkaen 59 € kuukaudessa | €59 in Finnish copy |
| Links | [See this week's schedule](url) | a raw URL · [click here](url) · [read more](url) |

- **Euros**: in English the symbol goes before the number with no space, and zero cents are
  dropped. Finnish (and Swedish and French) put it after the number with a space. Never two
  currencies in one piece, and never convert for the reader.
- **Link text names the destination**: a reader who sees only the link should know where it
  goes.
- **Dates and times are written in the reader's own conventions** (owner ruling, 13 Oct
  2026): English as UK English ("Monday 3 November", "4pm", "from 4pm to 6pm"), Finnish as
  Finnish ("ma 3.11.", "klo 16", "klo 16–18"), and Swedish and French as theirs.
- **No LaTeX or doubled dollar signs anywhere**, and currency symbols only for money.
