# 2. Audience map

*Excerpt of the School of Gaming Brand Voice & Identity Guidebook v2.0 (Jul 21, 2026), verbatim. The index in this folder's CLAUDE.md says what was left out.*

Four audiences, four registers. The single variable that changes most between them is lore density, which is why it gets its own section immediately after this one.

## 2.1 Gamers (roughly ages 7 to 15)

**Who they are.** Children and young teens who game already, who have a strong instinct for when an adult is faking enthusiasm, and who will not tolerate being talked down to. Many are here because it is the one place where the thing they are good at is treated as valuable.

**What they want from us.** To be in the story. To matter to the outcome. To be spoken to as a capable person with a real role.

**Register.** Second person, present tense, short sentences, concrete stakes. Direct address: "you", "your group", "your Gedu". Enthusiasm is fine, but earn it with specifics rather than adjectives. Never explain a joke. Never say "kids" or "children" to their face.

**Age split inside the band.** The 7 to 10 range needs shorter sentences, one instruction at a time, and clearer signposting of what to do next. The 11 to 15 range needs the opposite of condescension above all else, plus permission to be a little sardonic. When one piece has to serve both, write for the older half and make the structure obvious enough for the younger half to follow.

**Lore density: Level 3.** Full canon. No glossing, no translation, no winking at the fiction from outside it.

## 2.2 Parents and guardians

**Who they are.** Adults who love their child, are not sure gaming is good for them, and have often been told by other institutions that it is not. Many arrive carrying quiet guilt. Some arrive having already had a fight about it this week.

**What they want from us.** Reassurance that their child is safe. Evidence that something is being learned. A way to be involved that does not require them to become gamers themselves. Permission to stop feeling bad.

**Register.** Calm, specific, and adult. Never breathless. Never defensive. Lead with the concrete: who is supervising, what happens in 90 minutes, what changed in their child. Then, and only then, open the door to the story.

**The hard rule for parent copy.** Every lore term gets glossed on first use, in the same sentence, without an aside or a footnote. "Yty-Points, the points gamers earn for good work and good behavior" is correct. "Yty-Points (see our glossary)" is not.

**Lore density: Level 1, rising to Level 2** once they are enrolled and have been to a parents' evening.

## 2.3 Gedus and internal staff

**Who they are.** Gamers themselves, professionally trained, working from a lesson plan issued weekly by their Gedumaster. They are simultaneously the product, the frontline of the brand, and the people most likely to improvise.

**What they want from us.** Clarity about what to say, room to say it in their own voice, and a sense that they are inside the story rather than performing it.

**Register.** Peer to peer, practical, warm, and specific. Internal copy should give the reasoning, not just the rule, because Gedus have to adapt on the spot in front of twelve gamers and cannot phone a marketing team.

**Lore density: Level 3 in anything a gamer might see or overhear. Level 0 in operational documents.** A Gedu reading a safeguarding protocol at 8AM should not have to decode metaphor.

## 2.4 Partners, press, schools, municipalities, funders

**Who they are.** People assessing whether we are credible, safe, measurable, and worth associating with. Often they have never played a game and are quietly worried about looking foolish.

**What they want from us.** A defensible pedagogical model, a safeguarding story, evidence of scale and outcomes, and a concept they can explain to their own board in one sentence.

**Register.** Plain, confident, evidence-forward. **Lead with Human Skills**, which is vocabulary this audience already owns, and introduce the Four Yty-Elements second, as the structure underneath: a model of a child's relationship with self, others, society, and technology. Do not hide the fiction, but lead with the substance and let the fiction arrive as evidence of design intelligence rather than as decoration.

This is the one audience where naming the terminology choice itself is worth doing. Telling an education officer that we deliberately do not say "soft skills", and why, signals that we have thought about the pedagogy rather than borrowed a brochure.

**Lore density: Level 1.** Introduce Sogverse as our narrative architecture, name the Four Yty-Elements in full, and use the Hogwarts parallel freely. It works on this audience better than on any other.

## 2.5 Aspiring Gedus

**Who they are.** Adults considering Gedu Academy. Often gamers in their twenties, sometimes teachers looking for a way out of a system that exhausted them, sometimes our own graduated gamers. Many have been told their whole lives that the thing they are good at is a waste of time.

**What they want from us.** A real career with a visible ladder, proof this is a profession rather than gig work, and honesty about what the work actually demands.

**Register.** Adult to adult. Respectful of their existing skill. Direct about the standard. Gedu Academy's own copy already does this well: "A career, not a course" and "good Gedus are built, not found" are the register. Match it.

**The thing to never do.** Do not recruit adults with the same enchantment we use on children. An adult being sold a fantasy about a job feels manipulated. Tell them there are six levels, tell them Level 0 is free, tell them most people reach Level 1 in 6 to 12 weeks, and let the structure be the appeal.

**Lore density: Level 1.** They will live at Level 3 once they are inside. They do not start there.

## Audience quick reference

| Audience | Lore level | Sentence length | Leads with | Never |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Gamers | 3 | Short | Stakes and agency | Condescension, explained jokes |
| Parents | 1 to 2 | Medium | Safety and evidence | Hype, defensiveness, jargon |
| Gedus and staff | 3 or 0 | Medium | The why behind the rule | Vagueness, corporate tone |
| Partners and press | 1 | Medium to long | Pedagogy and safeguarding | Whimsy without substance |
| Aspiring Gedus | 1 | Medium | The ladder and the standard | Fantasy, hype, vagueness about pay |
