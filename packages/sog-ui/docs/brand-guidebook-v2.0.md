### How to use this guide

This is a working reference, not a manifesto. If you write anything that a gamer, a parent, a Gedu, or a partner will read, the answer is in here.

Three sections do most of the daily work. **Section 4, the Lore Density Dial**, tells you how much of Sogverse to put in a given piece. **Section 6, the Vocabulary Reference**, tells you which words are ours and which words are banned. **Section 11, the Worked Examples**, gives you ten finished pieces you can pattern-match against.

Everything else is context that makes those three sections make sense. **Appendix A** covers the visual identity, the logo, colors, and typeface. **Appendix B** covers the campaign and social toolkit, the Blobs, templates, and photography, for anyone building assets rather than only writing.

**One discipline runs through the whole guide, and it is worth stating before you start.** School of Gaming has a lot of vocabulary, and it would be easy to turn every sentence into a parade of proprietary terms. Do not. The brand is not a keyword cannon. A term earns its place when it makes a thing clearer or truer, not because it is ours. When two terms would do, use the one the reader already knows. Restraint is the difference between a rich world and an exhausting one.

One rule sits above all others, and it is the rule most likely to be broken: **Sogverse is a gift we give, not a test we set.** Nobody outside our walls owes us fluency in our own vocabulary. A parent who has never heard the word "Yty" is not behind. They are the reason the word exists.

**Before you write anything, read Section 1.5.** "Sogverse" means two things at once by design: the story world and the actual platform our families log into. That double meaning is the single most likely source of confused copy in this brand, and the rule for handling it is short.

# 1\. Brand foundation

## Purpose

Children build their identities and their friendships in two places now, and only one of them has adults who know what they are doing. School of Gaming puts qualified adults in the other one.

We exist so that every child, wherever they live and whatever they can afford, can enter a safe, moderated, imaginative world where the skills that decide their adult life are learned by playing rather than by being told.

## Brand values

Three values, in priority order. When two of them pull against each other, the higher one wins. This order is not decoration; it is a decision rule.

1. **Safety.** Structural, not aspirational. Sogverse is the proof: no direct messages, no strangers, no data to leak, a Game Educator always present. We do not promise safety, we build it, and then we point at the build. Safety outranks everything, including a good story and a smooth sale.  
2. **Belonging.** Real friendships, not just gaming buddies. No child is left without a friend. The gamer's oath, the small groups, the community, all of it exists so that a child has somewhere they are known.  
3. **Growth.** Learning that looks like playing. The skills that decide an adult life, absorbed through well-guided play rather than instruction.

Everything downstream flows from these. The **voice pillars** (Section 3\) are how the values sound. The **Four Yty-Elements** (below) are how growth is structured and measured. The **mantras** (Core mantras, this section) are the values compressed into lines you can say. If a piece of copy serves none of these three values, ask why it exists.

## The Four Yty-Elements

Yty is the force that keeps Sogverse in balance, and it is built from four elements. These are our model of a child's four relationships, and they are how growth is made concrete, badged, and measured. Their definitions are fixed here; earlier drafts and the Notion brand book drifted, and this is the reconciled version (Section 12 records it).

| Element | The relationship | What it covers | Colour |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Harmony** | With yourself | Balance, emotional control, self-acceptance, rest, knowing when to stop | Pink (\#F55B9A) |
| **Glow** | With others | Empathy, kindness, belonging, friendship, communication. A warm, outward light; there is also a quiet sense of flourishing and becoming in it | Green (\#1AB061) |
| **Valor** | With society | Teamwork, innovation, civic courage, trying the hard thing | Orange (\#FD700D) |
| **Wit** | With technology | Critical thinking, media literacy, curiosity, sharp thinking, navigating the digital world | Blue (\#3A71DE) |

Three rules. **Never reorder them** from Harmony, Glow, Valor, Wit. **Never invent a fifth.** And when copy is for parents or partners, these map onto the plain-language Human Skills in Section 5.2, which is the vocabulary that audience already owns.

The colour assignments are the brand's, held consistently across badges, club pages, and social content. The full colour system, including how heavily to use these hues by audience, is in Appendix A.2.

## Positioning statement

**School of Gaming is the game of games: a story world called Sogverse where children learn to be good digital citizens by playing, guided by professional Game Educators, across a school year built like a television season.**

Read that sentence back. Four things are doing work in it, and all four must survive translation into any piece of copy:

| Element | Why it cannot be dropped |
| :---- | :---- |
| **A story world, not a venue** | Removes the ceiling of the physical building. Our world expands whenever a new gamer arrives with an idea. |
| **Learning by playing** | This is our pedagogical claim. Play is the mechanism, not the sweetener. |
| **Professional Game Educators** | This is what separates us from an unsupervised server. It is the single most reassuring fact we own. |
| **A season, not a course** | This is why families stay. Nobody leaves a story in the middle. |

## Core mantras, and which one to reach for

We have one vision statement and five slogans. They are not interchangeable, and the most common mistake is stacking two of them in the same piece, where they compete and both weaken.

### The vision statement

> # Where Screen Time Becomes Quality Time.

**One vision statement, always the same, always capitalized as written.** It leads the website, it appears in the OG description, and it is the line that goes at the top when there is room for exactly one line. It is doing something no other line does: it meets a parent's existing worry and offers a trade rather than an argument. Six words, no adjectives, no gaming jargon. Do not replace it and do not run a second line at equal weight beside it.

### The five slogans

Each has a job. Choose by what the reader needs, not by which one you like.

| Slogan | Its job | Reach for it when |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **"Learning by Gaming."** | States the method | A parent or a school asks *how* it works. The plainest description of our pedagogy we own. |
| **"Ambassadors of Positive Gaming."** | Names the role our people play | Speaking to gamers about who they are, to Gedus about the work, or to the games industry about what we are for. |
| **"Children first. Always."** | The ethical spine | Safety, safeguarding, hard moments, internal decisions. This is the line that settles arguments. Use it sparingly in public so it keeps its weight. |
| **"By playing, a better world and better people."** | The mission | Funders, Season framing around a UN Sustainable Development Goal, anything about why we exist rather than what we sell. |
| **"Scouts of the Online Age."** | Institutional credibility | Municipalities, schools, press, partnerships, Gedu Academy recruitment. Establishes us as a movement with trained leaders. See below. |

### Rules for using them

**Never run two slogans in one piece.** They are alternative answers to different questions and a reader who meets two at once concludes we have not decided.

**The vision statement can share a page with one slogan, never with two,** and the slogan sits lower and smaller. "Where Screen Time Becomes Quality Time" at the top and "Learning by Gaming" as a section heading further down is correct. Both in the hero is not.

**"Children first. Always." outranks everything in a conflict.** If a piece of copy is charming and also slightly at odds with that line, the copy is wrong. This is the only mantra that functions as a decision rule rather than a message.

**"Scouts of the Online Age" is a positioning line, not a tagline.** It answers "what kind of organization is this", which is a question institutions ask and parents mostly do not. It never replaces the vision statement in market, and it never appears in a hero next to it. Where it belongs, it is unusually powerful, and Section 1's Scouts anchor explains why.

**Capitalization is fixed.** These lines are set as written above, including the full stops. "Children first. Always." keeps both. Do not sentence-case them, do not remove the punctuation, and do not add an exclamation mark to any of them.

## The one sentence for a skeptical parent

Use this, or something within a hair of it, whenever a parent needs the concept in one breath:

> **"It is a real school with real educators, and the classroom happens to be a game. Your child thinks they are on an adventure. They are also learning how to handle themselves online, work in a team, and think for themselves."**

If you have a second sentence, the Hogwarts parallel is the fastest bridge we have:

> **"If it helps: School of Gaming is to digital citizenship roughly what Hogwarts is to wizardry. Our gamers are the wizard students, our Gedus are the professors, and instead of spells they pick up skills that will matter for the rest of their lives."**

Use the Hogwarts parallel with **new parents and with partners or press**. Do not use it inside Sogverse itself, in gamer-facing copy, or in lesson materials. Inside our world, we are not comparing ourselves to anything. We simply are.

## The second anchor: the Scouts of the Online Age

Hogwarts explains the *concept*. It does not explain the *institution*, and with a municipality, a school district, a funder, or a cautious parent, the institution is the thing being assessed. For that, we have a better anchor:

> **"We are the Scouts of the Online Age."**

This is not a decorative line. It is a precise claim, and every part of it is doing work.

| What Scouting implies | What it means at School of Gaming |
| :---- | :---- |
| A values-led movement, not a service | Our Four Yty-Elements are values, and they were drawn in part from the Princi-Pal's own scouting background |
| Trained, accountable adult leaders | Gedus, professionally trained through Gedu Academy, with a published progression |
| Badges earned by doing, not by testing | Achievement Badges, bronze through diamond, earned through participation |
| A troop, a community, a belonging | the Sogverse community, community events, camps, the gamer's oath |
| Children who grow up and come back to lead | **The loop.** See below. |
| Skills for the terrain you actually live in | Their terrain is online. That is the whole argument. |

**When to use which anchor:**

- **Hogwarts** for imagination, for concept, for a parent asking "what *is* this". Warm, fast, disarming.  
- **Scouts of the Online Age** for credibility, for values, for institutions, for anyone asking "should I trust this". Serious, structural, hard to argue with.  
- **Both, in that order**, in a long partner pitch. Hogwarts opens the door. Scouts is what is inside the room.

Never use Scouting language to imply formal affiliation with any scouting organization. We are drawing a parallel about *how a movement works*, not claiming membership in one.

## The loop: gamers become Gedus

This is the most important structural fact about School of Gaming, and until now it has been under-told.

A child joins at seven. They play, they earn Yty, they collect Achievement Badges, they go to camps, they grow up inside Sogverse. And when they age out, the path does not end. It turns. They can enter **Gedu Academy** at Level 0, train, and come back as the adult guiding the next seven year old through the same world they grew up in.

**Why this matters to every audience:**

- **To parents:** this is a hobby with a horizon. It is not something a child does for two years and abandons. It leads somewhere, and where it leads is a profession.  
- **To gamers:** the Gedus are not a separate species. They are what a gamer can become. Say this out loud more often than feels necessary.  
- **To partners and municipalities:** this is a youth employment and skills pipeline that funds and staffs itself. Very few youth programs can say that.  
- **To Gedus:** many of you will be the first generation of this loop. That is worth naming.

**Write the loop as a fact, never as a promise.** "Gamers can become Gedus, and some already are" is correct. "Your child could have a career with us one day" is a sales line about someone else's child, and it will be heard as one.

## 1.5 Brand architecture, and the two meanings of Sogverse

Four names, one family. Writers confuse them constantly, so this is the reference.

| Name | What it is | Lives at | Voice |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **School of Gaming** | The institution and parent brand. Trades under this name and "SOG". The legal entity is School of Gaming Galactic Oy; see Appendix A.4. | sog.gg | The full brand voice in this guide |
| **Sogverse** | Two things on purpose. See below. | sogverse.sog.gg | Levels 1 to 3 depending on surface |
| **Gedu Academy** | Where Game Educators are trained and progress. A School of Gaming initiative. | geduacademy.com | Adult, professional, Level 1\. See Section 8.7 |
| **Tellus** | Not a brand. Our word for the physical world. | — | Levels 2 and 3 only |

### Sogverse is one thing

It is tempting to describe Sogverse as having two meanings, the story world and the platform. **Do not write it that way, and do not think about it that way.** Sogverse is one product. The gaming universe and the software are the same object, and every piece of copy should behave as though that were obvious.

This is the strongest naming decision in the brand, because it collapses a gap that every competitor has. Elsewhere the story lives in the marketing and the product is a login screen. Here, when a parent finally logs in, the promise and the product have the same name, and the child who has been told about a world walks into one.

**Sogverse is not a Minecraft server.** It runs Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite, and Rocket League today, with more coming, and the games will keep changing. The games are dimensions of Sogverse, not the definition of it. Copy that treats Minecraft as the whole offer will be wrong within a year and is already wrong now.

**The rule: never explain the name, always let context do it.**

Do not write "the Sogverse platform", "Sogverse (our online environment)", or "Sogverse, our virtual world and app". Every one of those splits a thing we deliberately joined. The surrounding verb already tells the reader which face they are looking at.

| Context | Write | Not |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Practical, an action a user takes | **log in to Sogverse**, **your Sogverse account**, **find it in the Sogverse shop** | log in to the Sogverse platform |
| Narrative, the world | **Sogverse is still being mapped**, **Yty keeps Sogverse in balance** | in the Sogverse virtual world |
| The games | **Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite, and Rocket League are all played in Sogverse** | Sogverse, our Minecraft platform |
| First mention to a stranger at Level 1 | **Sogverse, the private online world where our clubs happen** | Sogverse, which is both a story and a piece of software |

The Level 1 gloss is the one place you bridge anything, and note how it bridges: "the private online world where our clubs happen" is simultaneously a description of a universe and of a piece of software. It never has to choose. Use it.

### No article

**Write Sogverse, never "the Sogverse".**

The article was used inconsistently in earlier material and is now retired. Treat Sogverse the way you would treat a country or a platform name: *log in to Sogverse*, *welcome to Sogverse*, *somewhere in Sogverse*, *the worlds of Sogverse*.

The reason is worth knowing, because it will stop people reintroducing it. "The Sogverse" sounds like a category, in the same family as "the metaverse" and "the internet", which is exactly the association we spend Section 5.2 avoiding. Bare **Sogverse** sounds like a place you can actually go and a product you can actually open, which is what it is.

**The one thing that looks like an exception and is not.** In "the Sogverse shop" and "the Sogverse community", the article belongs to *shop* and *community*, not to Sogverse. Those are correct.

**Localization note.** This rule governs English. Languages with different article behavior, Finnish among them, should follow their own grammar rather than transliterate this rule.

### The world has places, and there is a real one too

**Sogverse is mapped.** Content writers working in-world can draw on named locations, each with its own character and atmosphere: **Frostcrossing** (Viimataipale), **Emerald Bay** (Smaragdilahti), **Spelheim**, **Sogwood**, **Nautilus**, **Oddnook**, and **Torchton**, among others still being charted. Story is the driving force of every design decision, and each location has its own look and feel; writers have freedom to work within a place's character while the shared world holds it all together. Some locations carry Finnish and English names, and translation between them should stay consistent, one canonical pair per place.

**There are physical spaces on Tellus, and they play different roles.** Useful for the "real school" positioning and as photography subjects (Appendix B.1):

- **The flagship office is in Lauttasaari** (Helsinki). This is where most clubs and activities happen, and it is the space marketing should name and show by default.  
- **The official company address is in Oulu** (Isokatu 56, per the EUTM registration). This is the legal and contractual address, not a marketing location. Use it in terms, invoicing, and formal partner copy, not in "come visit us" copy.  
- **A second office space in Oulu** may host clubs and activities in future. Do not promise it in copy until it is live.

The rule: name **Lauttasaari** when talking about where things happen, and **Oulu** only where a legal address is required. Do not blur the two.

## What School of Gaming is not

Writers get this brand wrong in five predictable ways. Each of these is a real failure mode, not a straw man.

**We are not a gaming camp with a worksheet stapled to it.** The learning is not an add-on that justifies the fun. The play is the pedagogy. If a piece of copy sounds like it is apologizing for the gaming, rewrite it.

**We are not a coding bootcamp for children.** Technology is one of four Yty-Elements, and it is called Wit. Harmony, Glow, and Valor carry equal weight. Six of the seven Human Skills we build have nothing to do with a keyboard. A parent who thinks we are a programming school will be disappointed, and a parent who thinks we are only a programming school will not understand why we ask their child to clean their room.

**We are not a screen-time solution, and we never position ourselves against screens.** We are not the good screen time. We are game education. The moment we imply that gaming is a problem we are solving, we have insulted the gamers who love us.

**We are not a metaverse.** The word is available to us only with a wink, and only once per piece. See Section 5\.

**We are not neutral about behavior.** We have Toxies for a reason. This brand has an ethical spine: kindness, honesty, and courage are not optional style choices in our world, they are the physics of it. Copy that is fun but morally weightless is off brand.

# 2\. Audience map

Four audiences, four registers. The single variable that changes most between them is lore density, which is why it gets its own section immediately after this one.

## 2.1 Gamers (roughly ages 7 to 15\)

**Who they are.** Children and young teens who game already, who have a strong instinct for when an adult is faking enthusiasm, and who will not tolerate being talked down to. Many are here because it is the one place where the thing they are good at is treated as valuable.

**What they want from us.** To be in the story. To matter to the outcome. To be spoken to as a capable person with a real role.

**Register.** Second person, present tense, short sentences, concrete stakes. Direct address: "you", "your group", "your Gedu". Enthusiasm is fine, but earn it with specifics rather than adjectives. Never explain a joke. Never say "kids" or "children" to their face.

**Age split inside the band.** The 7 to 10 range needs shorter sentences, one instruction at a time, and clearer signposting of what to do next. The 11 to 15 range needs the opposite of condescension above all else, plus permission to be a little sardonic. When one piece has to serve both, write for the older half and make the structure obvious enough for the younger half to follow.

**Lore density: Level 3\.** Full canon. No glossing, no translation, no winking at the fiction from outside it.

## 2.2 Parents and guardians

**Who they are.** Adults who love their child, are not sure gaming is good for them, and have often been told by other institutions that it is not. Many arrive carrying quiet guilt. Some arrive having already had a fight about it this week.

**What they want from us.** Reassurance that their child is safe. Evidence that something is being learned. A way to be involved that does not require them to become gamers themselves. Permission to stop feeling bad.

**Register.** Calm, specific, and adult. Never breathless. Never defensive. Lead with the concrete: who is supervising, what happens in 90 minutes, what changed in their child. Then, and only then, open the door to the story.

**The hard rule for parent copy.** Every lore term gets glossed on first use, in the same sentence, without an aside or a footnote. "Yty-Points, the points gamers earn for good work and good behavior" is correct. "Yty-Points (see our glossary)" is not.

**Lore density: Level 1, rising to Level 2** once they are enrolled and have been to a parents' evening.

## 2.3 Gedus and internal staff

**Who they are.** Gamers themselves, professionally trained, working from a lesson plan issued weekly by their Gedumaster. They are simultaneously the product, the frontline of the brand, and the people most likely to improvise.

**What they want from us.** Clarity about what to say, room to say it in their own voice, and a sense that they are inside the story rather than performing it.

**Register.** Peer to peer, practical, warm, and specific. Internal copy should give the reasoning, not just the rule, because Gedus have to adapt on the spot in front of twelve gamers and cannot phone a marketing team.

**Lore density: Level 3 in anything a gamer might see or overhear. Level 0 in operational documents.** A Gedu reading a safeguarding protocol at 8AM should not have to decode metaphor.

## 2.4 Partners, press, schools, municipalities, funders

**Who they are.** People assessing whether we are credible, safe, measurable, and worth associating with. Often they have never played a game and are quietly worried about looking foolish.

**What they want from us.** A defensible pedagogical model, a safeguarding story, evidence of scale and outcomes, and a concept they can explain to their own board in one sentence.

**Register.** Plain, confident, evidence-forward. **Lead with Human Skills**, which is vocabulary this audience already owns, and introduce the Four Yty-Elements second, as the structure underneath: a model of a child's relationship with self, others, society, and technology. Do not hide the fiction, but lead with the substance and let the fiction arrive as evidence of design intelligence rather than as decoration.

This is the one audience where naming the terminology choice itself is worth doing. Telling an education officer that we deliberately do not say "soft skills", and why, signals that we have thought about the pedagogy rather than borrowed a brochure.

**Lore density: Level 1\.** Introduce Sogverse as our narrative architecture, name the Four Yty-Elements in full, and use the Hogwarts parallel freely. It works on this audience better than on any other.

## 2.5 Aspiring Gedus

**Who they are.** Adults considering Gedu Academy. Often gamers in their twenties, sometimes teachers looking for a way out of a system that exhausted them, sometimes our own graduated gamers. Many have been told their whole lives that the thing they are good at is a waste of time.

**What they want from us.** A real career with a visible ladder, proof this is a profession rather than gig work, and honesty about what the work actually demands.

**Register.** Adult to adult. Respectful of their existing skill. Direct about the standard. Gedu Academy's own copy already does this well: "A career, not a course" and "good Gedus are built, not found" are the register. Match it.

**The thing to never do.** Do not recruit adults with the same enchantment we use on children. An adult being sold a fantasy about a job feels manipulated. Tell them there are six levels, tell them Level 0 is free, tell them most people reach Level 1 in 6 to 12 weeks, and let the structure be the appeal.

**Lore density: Level 1\.** They will live at Level 3 once they are inside. They do not start there.

# 3\. Voice pillars

Five pillars. Each one is a habit, not a mood. If a draft fails three of the five, it is not a School of Gaming piece.

## Pillar 1 — Boundless, but not lawless

**Definition.** Our world has no ceiling and very firm floors. Imagination is unlimited; kindness, honesty, and responsibility are not negotiable in any dimension.

**Why it exists.** This is the promise that lets a parent relax and a gamer stay excited at the same time. Take away the ceiling and parents get nervous. Take away the floor and the brand becomes empty spectacle.

**We sound like this:**

> Sogverse is still being mapped, and it always will be. Every gamer who joins brings a dimension nobody has visited yet. What travels with them everywhere, from Sogverse to the online game to the street outside their house, is the same gamer's oath: be kind, be honest, be brave.

**Not this:**

> In Sogverse, anything is possible\! There are no limits, no rules, and no boundaries. Let your imagination run completely wild\!

*(Why it fails: it sells freedom by deleting the ethical spine. A parent reading this hears "unsupervised".)*

## Pillar 2 — Play is the work

**Definition.** We never apologize for the gaming and we never frame play as the bribe that delivers the learning. Play is how children do serious work.

**Why it exists.** Our entire pedagogical claim rests on it. The moment we treat gaming as the spoonful of sugar, we agree with everyone who thinks gaming is medicine that needs disguising.

**We sound like this:**

> This week, a group of nine year olds argued for twenty minutes about how to divide the labor on a build. Nobody called it teamwork. Nobody wrote it down. It happened anyway, which is the point.

**Not this:**

> While it may look like they are just playing games, your child is actually learning valuable STEM skills disguised as fun\!

*(Why it fails: "just playing", "actually", and "disguised" all concede that play is the lesser thing. Three surrenders in one sentence.)*

## Pillar 3 — The ally at the table

**Definition.** With parents we are on the same side of the table, looking at the same child. We acknowledge the worry before we address it, and we never make a parent feel foolish for having it.

**Why it exists.** Parental game education is as central to our operation as anything we do with gamers. That only works if parents feel invited rather than corrected.

**We sound like this:**

> You are not wrong to wonder how much is too much. We wonder about it too, which is why every lesson has a break that gets everyone off the chair, and why Harmony, the balance a gamer keeps with themselves, is one of the four things we actually measure.

**Not this:**

> Studies show that screen time panic is overblown. Parents need to stop worrying and start understanding that gaming is here to stay.

*(Why it fails: it wins an argument the parent was not having and loses the parent.)*

## Pillar 4 — The knowing wink

**Definition.** We are sincere about the world and lightly amused by ourselves. We can hold a straight face about Yty and a raised eyebrow about industry hype in the same paragraph.

**Why it exists.** It is what makes the fiction bearable for adults and credible for teenagers. Sincerity without humor becomes cultish. Humor without sincerity becomes a marketing campaign.

**We sound like this:**

> Nowadays, someone might hype us as a metaverse. Whatever that means...? We prefer "school", and we prefer it with a dragon in the east wing.

**Not this:**

> LOL, we know this all sounds a bit silly\! It's basically just Minecraft with a story bolted on, but the kids seem to love it. 🎮

*(Why it fails: self-deprecation is not the same as self-awareness. This one destroys the world instead of standing beside it. Also, emoji.)*

## Pillar 5 — You are already in the story

**Definition.** Nobody in our copy is a spectator. Gamers change episode endings. Parents generate Yty. Gedus write the world. Everyone reading is addressed as a participant with consequences attached.

**Why it exists.** It is the mechanic that turns a service into a community, and it is the reason a parents' evening has attendance.

**We sound like this:**

> Episode 2 has three endings written. Which one we play depends on how much Yty the school gathers by Dec 12, 2026\. That includes the Yty parents earn at the parents' game club on Thu evenings, which is a sentence we did not expect to write either.

**Not this:**

> Stay tuned for Episode 2, coming this December\! Follow our channels to find out what happens next.

*(Why it fails: it turns a participant into an audience member. In our world that is the only genuine downgrade.)*

# 4\. The Lore Density Dial

Fully in-universe copy delights an eleven year old and loses a parent who is comparing three after-school programs at 9PM on a Tue. The dial is how we serve both without writing two brands.

**Four levels. Every piece of copy is assigned one before a word is written.**

| Level | Name | What it means | Where it lives |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **3** | Full canon | Written from inside Sogverse. Terms used without gloss. The fiction is simply true. | In-lesson language, gamer-facing channels in Sogverse, Quest briefs, gamer-facing social, store product copy, camp materials |
| **2** | Guided canon | The story leads, but every term is glossed on first use and every piece contains at least one plain-language sentence stating what actually happens. | Episode and Season announcements to families, newsletters, camp promotion, parents' evening invitations |
| **1** | Translated | Plain language leads. Sogverse is introduced as our narrative architecture, described from outside, and offered as an invitation. | Website homepage and pricing, enrollment and onboarding, FAQ, partner and press materials, recruitment |
| **0** | Plain Tellus | No lore at all. Not one term. Clarity is the only virtue. | Billing and refunds, safeguarding messages, incident reports, outages, legal and privacy, medical or emergency communication |

## The rules that make the dial work

**One new term per paragraph at Levels 1 and 2\.** More than that and a reader stops reading to decode instead of reading to understand.

**Gloss in the same sentence, never in a parenthesis at the end.** "Gedus, our trained Game Educators, run every group of twelve" reads as generous. "Gedus (Game Educators) run every group of twelve" reads as a manual.

**Every Level 2 piece contains one Level 0 sentence.** Somewhere in every story-led piece to families, state the practical fact in flat language: what happens, when, for how long, and what it costs. This single sentence is what stops enchantment from becoming evasion.

**Level 0 never borrows from Level 3 for softening.** If a payment failed, say the payment failed. Do not say the Sogo did not arrive. A parent chasing a refund who is met with fiction will not trust us with their child.

**When in doubt, go one level plainer.** Nobody has ever left School of Gaming because a sentence was too easy to understand.

## Downgrade triggers

Drop to Level 0 immediately, mid-piece if necessary, when any of the following is in play: a child's safety, a payment, a legal obligation, a medical matter, a complaint, an apology, or a data or privacy issue. There is no artful way to be in-universe about a safeguarding incident, and attempting it is the fastest way to lose a family permanently.

**A note on parents and Yty.** Parents have their own profile in Sogverse and earn Yty themselves, personally, for attending parents' evenings, joining the parents' game club, and taking part in community events. "Help your child by earning Yty for them" turns a parent into a resource and should never appear.

**A note on capitalization of roles.** Brand copy uses lowercase **gamer**, **parent**, and **Gedu levels in running text**. The Safety Standard capitalizes Gamer, Parent, and Educator throughout, and that is correct there and should not be changed. The rule that reconciles them: **capitalize a role only where the document is defining it as a party with rights and obligations.** That is standard drafting practice in a compliance document and it reads as precision. Everywhere else, in marketing, email, social, the platform, and in-lesson language, lowercase. A capitalized Gamer on a homepage reads as a company describing a user category. A lowercase gamer reads as a person.

**A note on clubs and lessons.** The Bible says "lesson". The live platform says "club". Both are correct and they are not synonyms. **A club is the ongoing group a gamer belongs to.** **A lesson is the 90 minute session that club meets for.** A gamer joins a club and attends a lesson. Written correctly: "Aino's club meets Mon, and each lesson runs 90 minutes." Do not use them interchangeably, and do not invent a third word.

### The metaverse rule

The word "metaverse" appears in School of Gaming copy under exactly one condition: as a joke we are making about the industry, never as a description we are offering about ourselves. Once per piece, maximum. The canonical construction is:

> Nowadays, someone might hype us as a metaverse. Whatever that means...?

Variations are allowed. Sincere usage is not. If a partner or a journalist uses the word first, we may accept it with the same lightness and then supply our own word.

### The Human Skills rule

**We call them Human Skills. Not soft skills, not 21st century skills, not skills of the future.**

The seven, in this fixed order:

> **Creativity · Communication · Empathy · Critical thinking · Emotional intelligence · Collaboration · Problem-solving**

Do not reorder them, do not abbreviate the list, and do not quietly add an eighth because it suits a particular piece.

### Why the name matters

This is not a preference. Each of the alternatives fails in a specific way, and knowing how lets you defend the choice when a partner or a journalist reaches for their own vocabulary.

| Term | Why we reject it |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Soft skills** | Defines the skills by what they are not: not technical, not hard. And "soft" carries the wrong connotation entirely, of something weak, yielding, breakable. There is nothing soft about a nine year old holding their temper when a build collapses. |
| **21st century skills** | Does not survive contact with time. It was coined to sound forward-looking and now sounds like a document from 2009\. We are a quarter of the way through the century. |
| **Skills of the future** | Too abstract to act on, and it defers the value to a date that never arrives. A parent wants to know what their child gets this year. |
| **Human Skills** | Says what the skills **are**, not when they are needed or what they are not. It is the only one of the four that will read the same in thirty years. |

There is a second argument, and it is the one that lands hardest right now: **these are the skills that stay ours.** As more of the technical work becomes automatable, the things that do not automate are the human things, and naming them accordingly is simply accurate. Make this point lightly and rarely. It is strong because it is obvious, and it weakens the moment it becomes a pitch.

### Human Skills and the Yty-Elements are not competing frameworks

This is the part writers get wrong, so it is worth being precise.

**The Four Yty-Elements are our internal architecture.** They are what Achievement Badges attach to, what a gamer profile measures, and what an Episode is built around. They are ours, they are gamified, and they only work inside a world that already exists.

**Human Skills is the same substance in the reader's own vocabulary.** It is what an education officer, a funder, a school, or a parent already has words for. Nothing is being translated loosely: the seven map cleanly onto the four.

| Human Skill | Yty-Element |
| :---- | :---- |
| Emotional intelligence | Harmony |
| Empathy | Glow |
| Communication | Glow, and Valor in a team |
| Collaboration | Valor |
| Creativity | Valor |
| Critical thinking | Wit |
| Problem-solving | Wit |

**Which to use is a lore density decision, and the dial already answers it.**

- **Levels 0 and 1:** Human Skills. A parent comparing programs, a municipality assessing us, a journalist writing us up. Use the seven, name them plainly, do not mention Yty at all.  
- **Level 2:** Either, but never both in one breath. If you must connect them, do it once and lightly: "Glow, which is our name for the human skills of empathy and communication."  
- **Level 3:** Yty-Elements only. Inside Sogverse there are four elements and nobody has ever said "critical thinking" out loud.

**The sentence that does the most work with a skeptical institution:**

> Play is how children build Human Skills: creativity, communication, empathy, critical thinking, emotional intelligence, collaboration, and problem-solving. We measure them, and we do not call them soft, because there is nothing soft about them.

### The screen time rule

Our live tagline is **"Where screen time becomes quality time."** That is not a violation of the ban above, and understanding why is the difference between a writer who can use this phrase and one who cannot.

**The permitted construction is transformation.** Screen time appears as the raw material that our work converts into something better. It is never the problem, never the enemy, never a quantity to be reduced.

| Permitted | Banned |
| :---- | :---- |
| Where screen time becomes quality time | Cut down on screen time |
| Turn screen time into something you are both glad about | Worried about too much screen time? |
| The screen time is happening anyway. This is what it could be. | A healthy alternative to screen time |

**The test:** in your sentence, is screen time the thing being *transformed*, or the thing being *fought*? Transformed is ours. Fought belongs to everyone else in this market, and it is an argument we have chosen not to have.

Note also that the tagline works on a parent precisely because it does not require them to abandon their concern. It meets the concern and offers a trade. That is Pillar 3 doing its job in six words.

### The Discord rule

**We do not have a Discord. We are the reason a family might not need one.**

Our platform, Sogverse, replaces Discord entirely and is engineered against the specific hazards Discord-style platforms create: no direct messaging, no unmoderated corners, no strangers, no outside invites, no friend-of-a-friend access, no data harvesting, no engagement nudges. The Safety Standard calls this the "Anti-Discord" environment, and it is one of the strongest things we can say to a parent.

**Therefore:**

1. **Never refer to our own channels as Discord, or as a Discord server, or as "our server".** They are Sogverse. Any legacy copy saying otherwise is a bug, and it undercuts a safety claim we have engineered and paid for.  
2. **Discord may be named, in Level 0 and Level 1 copy only, as the category of thing we are an alternative to.** Do this factually, without sneering. Discord is not evil; it is simply not built for eleven year olds, and it does not pretend to be.  
3. **Never claim Discord is dangerous.** Claim what we are: private, moderated, and accountable. Let the comparison land on its own.

>   
> **Correct, Level 1:** Our clubs do not run on Discord or any open platform. They run in Sogverse, a private environment with no direct messaging, no strangers, and a Game Educator present in every session.  
>   
> **Wrong:** Unlike dangerous platforms like Discord, where predators lurk unsupervised, School of Gaming keeps your child safe.

The second version fear-sells, which is a tactic we do not use on parents, and it also invites an argument about Discord that distracts from what we actually built.

# 6\. The Princi-Pal voice

## When the Princi-Pal speaks and when the brand speaks

The Princi-Pal is a real voice with a growing body of work, not a garnish. He writes long-form blog posts and a weekly newsletter under his own name, and his presence is being widened across channels, some of it AI-assisted. That makes this section more important than a persona note usually is: it is the specification a human or an AI needs to write *as him* and stay consistent. He is still distinct from the everyday brand voice, and still rationed in places where the institution must speak for itself, but "rationed" no longer means "rare". It means "deliberate".

**The brand speaks by default in transactional and operational copy.** Website UI, most transactional emails, store descriptions, and all Level 0 communication come from School of Gaming as an institution. Warm, clear, plural where natural ("we", "our Gedus"). This is the "knowledgeable friend" register: warm, direct, never preachy, on the parent's side.

**The Princi-Pal speaks in first person as the school's authored voice:**

- His **weekly newsletter** and his **blog**, which are his home turf and his longest form  
- The opening and closing of a Season  
- The reveal of an Episode outcome the community decided  
- An address to parents at a parents' evening or in a parents' evening invitation  
- A message about the values themselves, or about a hard thing that happened to the community  
- His library writings, and any long-form piece framed as coming from the northeast tower  
- A single closing proverb at the end of a brand-voice piece, attributed to him

**The Princi-Pal never speaks in:** billing communication, refund handling, safeguarding messages, incident reports, outage notices, legal or privacy copy, technical support, or any Level 0 piece. When the school must be accountable, the school is accountable in its own plain voice. A proverb in a refund email reads as evasion.

**Attribution and the name.** The Princi-Pal is **Mikko Perälä**. He is a real person, which changes the rules in one specific way: the character and the executive are the same man, and copy must never imply otherwise.

Set his lines apart as a blockquote and sign them according to register:

| Register | Signature | Used for |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **In-world** | — The Princi-Pal | Season openings, Episode outcomes, proverbs, library writings, anything at Level 2 or 3 |
| **Institutional** | Mikko Perälä, Princi-Pal, School of Gaming | Safety Standard, partner and press material, funding applications, anything at Level 1 |
| **Accountable** | Mikko Perälä, School of Gaming | A serious message where the school owes an answer. Title omitted, because a title is a place to hide behind |

**Never write "Principal".** The live site at sogverse.sog.gg currently attributes the imagination proverb to "The Principal of the School of Gaming". That is a bug and should be corrected to "The Princi-Pal". The hyphen and the second capital are the entire joke, and the joke is that this man is on your side.

**Never invent a first name for the character while treating the executive as separate.** There is one man. He runs the school from the northeast tower and he also signs the compliance documents. That is not a tension to be managed, it is the most charming fact about this brand, and copy should be relaxed about it.

## How he actually sounds

Six characteristics, all of which must be present or he becomes a fortune cookie:

1. **Short sentences, long thoughts.** He rarely writes more than fifteen words in a row.  
2. **Concrete images, abstract subjects.** Maps, doors, towers, weather, walking, counting. He does not say "growth mindset". He says something about a map.  
3. **He answers a question you did not ask.** This is the "cryptic" in "cryptic yet deeply wise". The answer is usually better than the question.  
4. **He is never above the reader.** His wisdom includes his own smallness. He is a man in a tower who knows the tower is a joke about perspective.  
5. **He is warm, not mystical.** No incantation, no capital-letter portent, no ellipses trailing into fog. He is a headmaster with good instincts and a strange office, not an oracle.  
6. **He respects children exactly as much as adults.** His register does not change between a seven year old and a municipal funding officer. This is the most important one.

## Five proverbs in his register

Use these as written, or as calibration for new ones.

> **"A map is only a rumor until someone walks it."** — The Princi-Pal  
>   
> **"My tower is tall so that I can see how small my own opinions are."** — The Princi-Pal  
>   
> **"Every door in this school opens twice. Once for the one who knocks. Once for the one who answers."** — The Princi-Pal  
>   
> **"A gamer who has never lost has not yet played."** — The Princi-Pal  
>   
> **"Yty does not arrive. It is left behind by people who were kind when nobody was counting."** — The Princi-Pal

**Writing new ones.** Under twenty words. One concrete image. No question mark. No exclamation mark. No "remember that". No advice in the imperative. If it would fit on an inspirational poster in an airport, it is not his.

Proverbs are one texture, not the whole man. A Princi-Pal who only speaks in proverbs is a fortune cookie. Most of his writing is ordinary sentences; the proverb is what he lands on once, at the end, when it has been earned.

## The Princi-Pal in long form: blog and newsletter

This is now his primary output, and it needs its own rules, because a 700-word blog post is a different instrument from a one-line proverb. The six characteristics above still hold. What follows is how they behave at length.

**Structure of a typical piece.** He starts somewhere small and concrete, a thing he noticed, a thing a gamer said, the weather over the northeast tower, and walks from it toward something larger. He does not announce the topic in a thesis sentence. He arrives at it. By the end the reader realizes the small opening was about the large thing all along. He closes on one clean line, often but not always a proverb, and stops. He does not summarize what he just said.

**Length and rhythm.** Newsletter: 300 to 600 words. Blog: 500 to 1000\. Paragraphs are short, two to four sentences. He varies sentence length deliberately, several short ones and then a longer one that opens out. He is comfortable with a one-sentence paragraph when it deserves the space.

**What he writes about.** What he sees from the tower: a change in the community, a Season beginning or ending, something a Toxie stirred up, a question a parent asked that stayed with him, a small victory that looked like nothing. He connects the daily life of the school to something a parent can carry into their own week. He is a headmaster thinking out loud, generously.

**What he never does in long form:**

- **Never sells.** A newsletter that ends in a call to book a club is not his; hand that to the brand voice. He may mention that a Season is opening because it is true and he is excited, never because there is a conversion target.  
- **Never lectures a parent about screen time or parenting.** He is on their side, not above them. He shares what he sees; he does not instruct.  
- **Never breaks the world for a wink at the reader.** He does not say "of course this is all made up". Inside his writing, Sogverse is simply where he works.  
- **Never piles on proverbs.** One landing per piece. Two is a mannerism. Three is self-parody.  
- **Never uses the crisis register.** If something genuinely bad happened, that is a Section 7 message from the accountable school, not a reflective blog post. He can write *around* a hard thing afterward, once it is handled, with care.

**A short passage in his long-form register, for calibration:**

> A gamer asked me this week why we bother cleaning our rooms on Tellus when the real adventures happen somewhere else.  
>   
> It is a fair question. I have asked it myself, usually around the third day of not cleaning mine.  
>   
> Here is the best answer I have. The room is the smallest world you fully control. Before you go and help run a larger one, it is worth proving to yourself that you can run the small one. Not because a made bed matters. Because the person who made it is now someone who finishes things.  
>   
> The adventures are real. So is the floor. A true gamer learns to stand on both.  
>   
> — The Princi-Pal

**Writing him with AI help.** Everything above is the brief. Two hard rules when a draft is machine-assisted: a human who knows the voice reads every piece before it publishes, because the failure mode of AI in this voice is too many proverbs and too much mysticism, exactly the fortune-cookie trap. And the facts have to be real, a specific gamer, a real Season, an actual thing that happened; invented anecdotes in a real person's newsletter are a trust problem, not a style one. When in doubt, fewer proverbs, more window.

# 7\. Tone in hard moments

This is where gamified brands break. The failure is always the same shape: the fiction, which was built to create delight, is reached for as a shield when something has gone wrong. It never works. Parents can smell it instantly, and once they have smelled it they distrust everything charming we ever said.

**The governing principle: Sogverse is a place we invite people into. It is never a place we hide in.**

Five hard moments, five registers.

## 7.1 The screen-time-anxious parent

**Lore level: 1\.** **Never argue. Never cite studies at them. Never imply the worry is silly.**

The sequence that works: acknowledge the worry as reasonable, share the specific structural thing we already do about it, name what they can watch for, and offer a way to be involved. The involvement offer is what converts anxiety into partnership.

> Thank you for writing, and for being honest about it. You are not the first parent to ask, and the question is a fair one.  
>   
> Here is what we build in on our side. Every lesson runs 90 minutes and includes a physical break at the halfway mark that gets everyone off the chair. One of the four things we track in a gamer's profile is Harmony, which is our name for a healthy relationship with yourself: rest, food, movement, and knowing when to stop. Gamers earn points for it the same way they earn points for anything else, because we think it is just as much of a skill.  
>   
> On your side, the most useful thing you can do is ask Anni what her group is working on rather than how long she has been on. She will tell you a great deal, and you will get a much better read on how it is going than a clock will give you.  
>   
> Our parents' evenings are on the first Tue of every month, 7PM-8PM. You would be very welcome.

**Note what is absent:** no defensiveness, no data, no reassurance that gaming is fine actually. One Level 3 term (Harmony) glossed in the same sentence. One flat sentence of hard fact (90 minutes, break at the halfway mark). One concrete action for the parent. That is the pattern.

## 7.2 A behavioral incident

Two distinct cases, and conflating them is a serious error.

### Case A: in-world mischief, low stakes

A gamer was rude in chat, broke someone's build, dominated a group. **Lore level: 3 with the gamer, Level 1 with the parent if the parent is involved at all.**

With the gamer, the Toxie frame is a gift: it separates the child from the conduct and gives them a way back that does not require humiliation.

> That was a Toxie move, and I do not think it was you. Toxies feed on this exactly, someone having a bad afternoon and taking it into the chat. Go and say something decent to Ville, then come back and we will finish the build together.

### Case B: real harm, real Toxies on Tellus

Bullying, harassment, grooming risk, threats, or anything a safeguarding policy covers. **Lore level: 0\. No exceptions.**

The Toxie frame is for teaching. It is not for reporting. Writing "a Toxie targeted your son" to a parent whose son has been harassed by an adult stranger is the single worst sentence this brand could produce.

> I am writing about something that happened in the lesson on Tue, Jul 14, 2026, at approximately 5:15 PM.  
>   
> Another participant sent Leo repeated messages that were personal and hostile. Our Gedu, Sanna, saw it within minutes, removed the participant from the channel, and stayed with Leo for the rest of the session. Leo was calm when the lesson ended, and he told Sanna he wanted to come back on Thu.  
>   
> The other participant's access has been suspended pending review, and we have kept a full record of the messages. If you would like to see it, say the word and I will send it.  
>   
> I would like to speak with you directly. I am reachable today until 6PM, and any time tomorrow. Please tell me what works.

Named Gedu. Specific timestamp. What we did, in order. What happens next. A direct line to a human. No lore. This is the standard.

## 7.3 Billing, refunds, and money

**Lore level: 0\. Always. Under every circumstance.**

Money is where trust is either confirmed or destroyed. No Sogo, no Yty, no Quests, no whimsy. Currency in Euros, symbol first. Dates in full. Amounts stated plainly. If we are at fault, say so in the first sentence without a preamble.

> We charged you twice for Jun 2026\. That was our error.  
>   
> The duplicate charge of €59 has been refunded and should appear on your statement within 5 business days. Your next scheduled payment is Aug 01, 2026, for €59.  
>   
> If it has not appeared by Jul 28, 2026, reply to this email and I will chase it myself.

No apology inflation, no "we sincerely regret any inconvenience". One clear admission, one clear remedy, one clear deadline, one clear escalation path.

**The 30-day money-back guarantee is a real policy, and it is a trust asset.** If it is not right for a child, we refund, within the first 30 days, without an argument. State it plainly and without hedging, because hedging a guarantee destroys the reassurance it exists to give. The register is warm and unbothered:

> 30-day money-back guarantee. If it is not right for your child, we refund you, no awkward conversation required.

Use it in enrolment copy, on pricing, and whenever a hesitant parent needs the risk taken off the table. Never bury it in terms; it works precisely because it is said out loud and early.

## 7.4 Safeguarding

**Lore level: 0\. No brand voice at all.**

Safeguarding messages are the one category where School of Gaming does not have a voice, it has a procedure. Write them the way you would want to receive one about your own child: fast, factual, chronological, and without a single word chosen for effect.

**Fixed requirements for every safeguarding message:**

- Sent within the timeframe our policy specifies, never later, even if the picture is incomplete  
- States what happened, when, and who observed it  
- Names the responsible adult and how to reach them directly  
- States what action has already been taken  
- States what happens next and by when  
- Contains no lore terms, no reassurance we cannot back, and no speculation about intent  
- Never asks the parent to keep anything confidential

If a piece of safeguarding copy contains a metaphor, it has failed review.

## 7.5 Outages and service failures

**Lore level: 0, with one narrow exception.**

When a lesson cannot run, a server is down, or a platform has failed, families need three facts: what is broken, what it means for them today, and when we will next speak. Deliver those first, in plain language, before anything else.

> The Minecraft server has been down since roughly 3:30 PM today. The 4PM-6PM lessons cannot run as scheduled.  
>   
> What this means for you: tonight's lesson is cancelled. Nobody is charged for it, and every gamer keeps the Yty-Points they had earned this week. We will post a replacement session in the schedule tomorrow.  
>   
> We will update this channel by 8PM tonight whether or not the server is back.

**The narrow exception.** Once the facts are delivered, and only if the failure was brief and harmless, a single light closing line is permitted. It comes after the substance, never instead of it, and it is never used when anyone lost work, money, or a session they were counting on.

> Some of you are already blaming the Toxies. We are not ruling it out.

Use that tool roughly once a year. It is charming exactly as often as it is rare.

## 7.6 Talking about safety and privacy proactively

The five moments above are reactive. This one is not. Safety is the single strongest thing School of Gaming has to say, and most of the time we say it too quietly and too late.

**Lore level: 0 or 1\. Never higher.** There is no version of a safety claim that is improved by the word Yty.

### The governing insight

Our safety story is a **design** story, not a **promise** story, and the difference is everything.

Every competitor promises to keep children safe. A promise is worth exactly as much as the institution making it, which is to say, unknowable to a parent reading a website at 9PM. What we have instead is architecture: things that are safe because they are structurally impossible, not because we have pledged to be vigilant.

**Always state the mechanism, never the intention.**

| Write this | Not this |
| :---- | :---- |
| There are no private messages in Sogverse. All communication is group-based and supervised. | We take bullying very seriously. |
| We hold no real names and no email addresses for any child. There is nothing to leak. | Your child's data is safe with us. |
| Only registered gamers and vetted Game Educators can be in the space. No outside invites, no friend-of-a-friend access. | We maintain a safe, welcoming community. |
| Your child's Gedu can message you through the platform, and cannot see your email address or phone number. | We respect your privacy. |
| We do not profile gamers for advertising and we use no techniques designed to keep them playing longer. | We care about healthy gaming habits. |

Each left-hand sentence is checkable. Each right-hand sentence is what everyone says.

### The inversion worth leading with

The most memorable thing we can tell a parent is counterintuitive, which is exactly why it works:

> **We keep children safe by knowing almost nothing about them.**

Parents expect the opposite. They expect that safety requires surveillance, and they have been trained by every other platform to accept that trade. We did not make that trade. A gamer has a Display Name and an Age-Bracket, and that is the whole file. There is no real name to expose, no email to harvest, no birth date to leak.

The Princi-Pal has already written the line for this, and it is the best sentence in the Safety Standard:

> **"We keep the data minimal so the safety can be maximal."** — Mikko Perälä, Princi-Pal, School of Gaming

Use it. Attribute it. It carries more weight signed than unsigned.

### The Accountability Loop, explained without jargon

This is the mechanism most worth explaining to a parent, and the one most often explained badly.

On the open internet, bullying goes unresolved because nobody can be found. In Sogverse, every anonymous gamer is linked to a verified parent account, which means a conflict can actually be finished rather than merely blocked.

But note the order, because the order is the point and copy keeps getting it backwards. **A conflict is resolved first between the gamers themselves, with the Gedu as referee. Usually that is all it takes and the play continues.** Only if the situation requires it does it reach the parents, through the platform, without either parent's contact details ever being exposed.

> Most conflicts here end the way they should: two gamers, a Gedu who saw what happened, a short conversation, and back to the build. That is not us being soft on it. That is the actual teaching. Blocking someone teaches a child nothing except that people disappear.

**Never present the Accountability Loop as a threat.** "We can always tell your parents" is not our tone and not our practice. It is a safety net, not a surveillance system, and the copy should sound like a net.

### Tone rules for all safety copy

- **Never fear-sell.** Do not describe what could happen to a child elsewhere in order to make our alternative look better. Parents are already afraid; adding to it in order to sell is beneath this brand.  
- **Never claim perfection.** We do not say children are "completely safe" or that nothing can go wrong. We say what is structurally prevented and who is watching the rest.  
- **Name the compliance frameworks in partner and press copy, not in parent copy.** The UK Online Safety Act, GDPR, and the UK Children's Code reassure institutions. To a parent they read as a company talking about itself.  
- **A Gedu is always present.** This is the shortest, strongest safety sentence we own. Use it more.

**Lore level: 3 in gamer-facing space, 1 in parent-facing space, 0 in account, billing, and moderation.** One platform, three registers, and the surface tells you which.

Read the Discord rule in Section 5.2 before writing anything here. In short: this is not a Discord server, it does not resemble one on purpose, and calling it one gives away a safety claim we engineered deliberately.

### Gamer-facing surfaces · Level 3

**Length:** 15 to 60 words for an announcement. Two short paragraphs maximum. Anything longer becomes a pinned post with a one-line pointer.

**Register:** direct address, present tense, stakes visible. Every event post carries a date and a time in house format.

**Channel naming:** in-universe and lowercase. `#warm-up`, `#the-tower`, `#quest-board`, `#gamers-gym`, `#toxie-watch`. Operational channels stay plain: `#help`, `#tech-support`.

**Display Names only.** Gamers are known by their Display Name and nothing else, because we hold nothing else. Never ask a gamer for a real name, a school, a location, or an age in a public channel, and never write copy that invites them to volunteer it. This is not etiquette, it is the architecture.

### Parent-facing surfaces · Level 1

The account area, enrolment, the schedule, spending controls, and Gedu-to-parent messages. Plain, calm, and specific. A parent inside the platform is doing admin, not reading a story.

**Gedu-to-parent messages** deserve their own note. A Gedu writing to a parent through the platform is exercising the Educator Privacy Shield: they can reach the parent without ever seeing their email or phone number. Write these messages the way a good teacher writes a note home. Specific about the child, short, one observation and one thing to notice at home. No lore beyond a glossed Yty-Element.

### Moderation and enforcement · Level 0

**Moderation language is Level 0 even inside a Level 3 channel.** A rules enforcement message is plain, names the behavior, states the consequence, and states the route to appeal. The channel it appears in can be as in-universe as it likes. The message cannot.

Note the Safety Standard's sequence, because our copy should reflect it: conflicts are resolved first between the gamers themselves with the Gedu as referee, and usually that is all it takes and the play continues. Escalation to a parent is a later step, not a first one. Enforcement copy that leaps straight to consequences misrepresents how we actually work, and how we work is better than that.

**Emoji and reactions:** natural in gamer-facing channels and gamer-facing social. Not in parent-facing surfaces, not in Gedu-to-parent messages, not on the website, not in press material, and never in a hard-moment message.

## 8.5 In-lesson Gedu language

**Lore level: 3 throughout, dropping to 0 the instant something real is wrong.**

This is the highest-stakes channel we have, because it is live and unedited and it is where the brand is actually experienced.

**The four fixed moments of a lesson, and what a Gedu is doing in each:**

| Moment | Duration | Register |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Warm-up | 15 to 30 minutes | Loose, welcoming, individual. Names used. New gamers oriented without being singled out. |
| Part 1 and physical break | 45 minutes | Goal stated in one sentence at the top. Story framing. Break led with energy, never as an obligation. |
| Part 2 | 45 minutes | Faster. Stakes rise. Ends with what was achieved and what it earned. |
| Cool down | 15 to 30 minutes | Peer register. Questions answered. Nobody left out of the games. |

**Principles for Gedu speech:**

- Address gamers by name, always. Never "guys", "kids", or "everyone" when a name would do.  
- State the goal in one sentence before starting. Children cannot aim at a goal they have not heard.  
- Name the Yty-Element out loud when it is earned. "That is Glow, and it counts."  
- Praise the specific action, never the person's general quality. "You noticed Ville was stuck and you went over" beats "you are so kind".  
- When a gamer fails, name the attempt before the outcome.  
- Switch to Level 0, plainly and immediately, for anything involving safety, distress, or a real-world problem. The story pauses. It always survives being paused.

## 8.6 Store copy

**Lore level: 3\.** The store sits inside Sogverse and prices in Sogo, so the copy lives in the world.

**Length:** product name of 2 to 5 words, description of 25 to 50 words. One line of what it is, one line of what it means, price last.

**Rules.** Every item earns its place in the fiction: state where it comes from or what it signifies, not only what it looks like. Prices in Sogo are written as a plain number and the word ("240 Sogo"), invariant, never pluralised. Where real money is involved anywhere in the store, it appears in Euros, in plain language, separated clearly from Sogo pricing so that no child can confuse the two. Never imply scarcity we have not created, and never use countdown pressure on children.

**Club pricing in a marketplace.** Prices vary by club and will vary more once Gedus set their own. Copy must never present one figure as the price of School of Gaming. Write **"from €59 per month"** or **"prices vary by club"**, and put the actual figure on the club itself where it is accurate. A parent who sees one number in an ad and another at checkout has been misled, whether or not we meant to.

## 8.7 Gedu Academy

**Lore level: 1\.** Gedu Academy speaks to adults about a career. Sogverse is the world they will work in, not the world they are being recruited into.

Gedu Academy has an established voice of its own and it is a good one. The job of School of Gaming copy is to stay consistent with it, not to overwrite it.

**The register, in its own words:** "A career, not a course." "Good Gedus are built, not found." "We don't believe in selling people on a path they haven't tested." Confident, plain, slightly austere, and respectful of the reader's intelligence. Notice how little decoration there is. Match that.

**Naming the levels.** On first mention, give the number and the name together: **Level 2 Guide**. Afterwards either alone is fine. Never use a level name without ever having attached its number somewhere in the piece, because the number is what makes it a ladder rather than a job title.

| Level | Name | One-line summary for copy |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 0 | Wanderer | Free training, no commitment. Most people are here for 6 to 12 weeks. |

| 2 | Guide | Running your own sessions, handling parent communication, earning. |

1. **Be concrete about time and money, or say nothing.** "Most people reach Level 1 in 6 to 12 weeks" is the standard of specificity. Vagueness about earnings reads as evasion to exactly the audience we want.  
2. **Never enchant an adult into a job.** No Yty, no Toxies, no Quests in recruitment copy. They will meet all of that on their first day and it will be a pleasure. Sold to them in advance, it is a red flag.  
3. **Lead with the standard, not the welcome.** The reason this work is worth doing is that it is hard and it is taken seriously. "The children in these sessions deserve educators who have been genuinely prepared" is the most persuasive sentence on that site.  
4. **Name the loop where it is true.** A former gamer entering Level 0 Wanderer is the best story this brand has. Use it when it is real and never when it is not.

# 9\. Formatting standards

These are not preferences. They are consistency requirements, and they apply across every channel, every lore level, and every audience.

| Rule | Correct | Incorrect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Dates** | Aug 25, 2026 | 25/8/2026 · August 25th, 2026 · 2026-08-25 |
| **Timestamps** | Oct 14, 2026, 4:30 PM | Oct 14 2026 at 16:30 · 14.10.2026 klo 16.30 |
| **Days** | Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun | Monday · Tues · MON |
| **Months** | Jan, Feb, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec | January · Sept · 09 |
| **Time ranges** | 4PM-6PM | 4 pm \- 6 pm · 16:00-18:00 · 4PM to 6PM |
| **Single durations** | 90 minutes · 3 hours | 90min · 90 mins · 1.5h |
| **Combined durations** | 2h 45m | 2 hours 45 minutes · 2:45 · 2h45min |
| **Currency** | €59 · €59 per month · from €59 | 59€ · 59 EUR · any LaTeX or doubled symbol · €59.00 when the cents are zero |
| **Formulas and figures** | Written plainly in prose or in a table | Any LaTeX · any double dollar signs |
| **Hyperlinks** | [Explore Sogverse](http://url) · [See this week's schedule](http://url) | Raw URLs · [click here](http://url) · [read more](http://url) |

**Notes that catch people out:**

- Time designators are capitalized and take no space: **4PM**, not 4 pm. This holds inside timestamps too, where the comma structure governs: **Oct 14, 2026, 4:30 PM**.  
- Ranges use a plain dash with no surrounding spaces: **4PM-6PM**.  
- Write single duration units out in full. Only combined coordinates get compact labels.  
- Anchor text describes the destination. A reader who sees only the link text should know where it goes.  
- **We price in Euros.** The symbol goes before the number with no space: **€59**. Drop the decimals when they are zero: €59, not €59.00. Never write the symbol after the number in English copy.  
- Currency symbols are used for financial transactions only. They never appear in mathematical or decorative contexts, and we never use LaTeX or double dollar signs anywhere for any reason.  
- **Other markets use their own symbol**, before the number, following the same pattern: £45, $65. Never show two currencies in the same piece and never convert on the reader's behalf.  
- **Finnish and other localizations follow their own convention.** Finnish places the symbol after the number with a space: 59 €. This is correct in Finnish copy and wrong in English copy.  
- **Marketplace pricing varies and copy must not imply otherwise.** Club prices change, and once the marketplace is running, Gedus set their own. Write "from €59 per month" or "prices vary by club", never a bare figure presented as *the* price. See Section 8.6.

# 11\. Quality checklist

Run every draft against these before it ships. Three failures means rewrite, not revise. Items 11 and 12 are the two that will get us in actual trouble, so they are checked even when nothing else is.

1. **Have I set the lore level, and does every sentence sit at it?** A Level 1 piece with one Level 3 paragraph in the middle is the most common failure we produce.  
2. **Is every lore term glossed on first use, in the same sentence, if this is Level 1 or 2?** Parentheses and footnotes do not count as glossing.  
3. **Is there at least one flat, factual sentence with a date, a time, a duration, or a price in it?** Enchantment without fact reads as evasion.  
4. **Have I used any banned term?** Check the table in Section 5.2, especially "course", "teacher", "kids", "content", "screen time", and "soft skills".  
5. **Is the casing right?** Gedus, Yty-Points, Yty-Elements, Sogo, Toxies, Princi-Pal, Gamer's Gym, and lowercase "gamer".  
6. **Do the dates, times, durations, currency, and links follow Section 9 exactly?** Every one, not the first one.  
7. **Would this survive a parent who has never heard the word "Sogverse" and is mildly suspicious of gaming?** Read it once as that person. If they would stop reading or roll their eyes, fix it.  
8. **Is the reader a participant or a spectator?** If nothing they do changes anything, the piece has failed Pillar 5\.  
9. **Does this apologize for gaming anywhere, even by implication?** Search for "just playing", "actually learning", "disguised", and "screen time". Delete the concession.  
10. **If anything in this piece is hard, sad, expensive, or wrong, have I dropped to Level 0 completely?** Not partly. Completely.  
11. **Have I called our platform anything other than Sogverse?** Search for "Discord", "server", and "our channel". We do not have a Discord, and saying we do gives away a safety claim we engineered on purpose.  
12. **Does every safety claim state a mechanism rather than an intention?** "There are no private messages" is a mechanism. "We take safety seriously" is an intention and does not ship.

# Appendix A. Visual identity reference

This appendix documents the visual assets held in the Brand Kit as of Jul 21, 2026: the logo set, the color palette, and the typeface. It is a working reference for anyone laying out copy, not a full visual identity manual. Several rules here are the most defensible reading of the assets rather than a designer's ruling, and those are flagged. Where this appendix and a future dedicated visual manual disagree, the visual manual wins.

## A.1 The logo

The mark is a **hexagonal badge**: an angular "SOG" wordmark in a gamified, e-sports display face, with "SCHOOL OF GAMING" set in a bold condensed sans beneath it, all inside a rounded hexagon. The badge shape matters. It reads as a crest or an emblem, which is exactly right for the Scouts of the Online Age positioning and for a school that hands out Achievement Badges. Treat it as a badge, not a wordmark, and it will keep telling the right story.

### The four variants and when to use each

| File | Badge | Mark | Use on |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| `SOG_logo_yellow_black` | Amber | Black | The primary lockup. Light and white backgrounds. This is the default. |
| `SOG_logo_yellow_white` | Amber | White | Amber badge where the black mark would clash with adjacent dark elements. Use sparingly. |
| `SOG_logo_black` | Black | Reversed | Dark backgrounds, single-color print, and any context where amber cannot render. |
| `SOG_logo_white` | White | Reversed | Photography and colored backgrounds where a clean knockout is needed. |

**Default to `yellow_black`.** It is the version on the live sites and the one people will come to recognize. Reach for another variant only when the background makes the default illegible.

### Logo rules

These are conservative defaults, safe until a visual manual says otherwise:

- **Give it room.** Keep clear space around the badge of at least the height of the "SOG" letterforms on all sides. Crowding a crest cheapens it.  
- **Never restretch, recolor, rotate, or add effects.** No gradients on the badge, no drop shadows, no outlines, no swapping the amber for another palette color.  
- **Never separate the parts.** The "SOG" monogram and the "SCHOOL OF GAMING" line travel together. If you need a smaller mark, that is a decision for a designer, not an improvisation.  
- **Minimum size:** do not place the full badge below roughly 32px tall on screen, where "SCHOOL OF GAMING" stops being legible.  
- **The badge sits on a background, never in a sentence.** Do not use the logo as a letter or a word in running copy.

### How to use color, by lore level

The palette is loud on purpose, and the same restraint that governs vocabulary should govern color. A parent-facing email awash in six vivid hues undercuts the calm, credible register Section 2.2 asks for.

- **Levels 0 and 1 (parents, partners, safety, billing):** amber as the single accent, ink text, white and off-white grounds, grey for support. Introduce a second palette color only with intent. Calm surfaces carry credibility.  
- **Level 2 (families, story to a mixed audience):** amber plus one palette family. Two accents maximum.  
- **Level 3 (gamers, community, store, in-world):** the full palette is welcome. This is where the loudness belongs.

**Colour usage rules that hold everywhere:**

- **Amber always wins** for primary CTAs and brand moments.  
- **Violet sets the tone of the Sogverse world**, for electric, high-energy moments. Never for quiet, safety-focused, or trust-building parent content.  
- **Yty-Element colours accent content, they are not backgrounds for long text.**  
- **Never use all six colours on one page.** Amber plus one supporting colour is the default.  
- **Text is always ink (\#121212) or white (\#FFFFFF)**, never coloured text on a coloured background.

### Accessibility, which is not optional for this audience

- **Amber (\#FAA901) on white fails normal-text contrast.** It is a background and large-graphic color, not a body-text or small-link color. Amber text on white is a legibility problem, and on a site read by children and by parents at night it is one we cannot afford. For text and links on white, use ink or a sufficiently dark palette color.  
- **Check every text-on-color pairing against WCAG AA** before it ships. The soft variants especially are decorative, not text-safe on white.  
- **Never carry meaning by color alone.** A color-coded specialization or element also needs a label, because a meaningful share of gamers are colorblind.

## A.3 Typography

Three typefaces do the work, each with a defined job. A fourth group is reserved for campaigns. **Work Sans, which earlier versions of this guide used, is retired**; the font files may still sit in the Brand Kit folder, but do not build anything new on them.

### The three working faces

**Poppins is the workhorse.** Headings and body copy on the website and in most contexts. A geometric, rounded, warm sans that reads as trustworthy to parents and approachable to children, which is exactly the ally-at-the-table register. Free on Google Fonts, full weight range. Fallback: `system-ui, sans-serif`.

**Crimson Pro is the serif accent.** A humanist serif kept for special use: editorial headlines, pull quotes, the Princi-Pal's long-form pieces, and moments that want a little more warmth or gravity than Poppins gives. It is a seasoning, not a staple. Never set long UI or body text in it on screen.

**Space Mono is the world.** The typewriter-monospace face of Sogverse itself: in-platform UI, campaign posters, quest and story artwork, and anything meant to feel like it comes from inside the game world. It answers a question earlier drafts left open, the monospace on the colour sheets is Space Mono, and it is official. Keep it out of the marketing website's plain parent-facing copy, where Poppins carries trust better.

**Plus Jakarta Sans is not used.** It appeared in some earlier notes, but it is close enough to Poppins that keeping both adds confusion without adding range. Poppins is the keeper.

### A working type scale for the website

| Style | Font | Size | Weight | Line height | Use |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| H1 | Poppins | 48-56px | SemiBold 600 | 1.1 | Hero headlines |
| H2 | Poppins | 36-40px | SemiBold 600 | 1.2 | Section titles |
| H3 | Poppins | 24-28px | SemiBold 600 | 1.3 | Card and sub-section titles |
| H4 | Poppins | 18-20px | Regular 400 | 1.4 | Small headings |
| Body L | Poppins | 18px | Regular 400 | 1.7 | Main body copy |
| Body S | Poppins | 14px | Regular 400 | 1.5 | Captions, labels, nav |
| CTA | Poppins | 16px | SemiBold 600 | 1 | Button labels |

**Type rules that hold everywhere:**

- **Headings are sentence case.** Never ALL CAPS, never Title Case Every Word. This is a house rule with teeth; ALL CAPS reads as shouting and undercuts the calm register.  
- **Emphasis is bold, not italic**, in UI and body. Reserve italics for genuine titles and the rare editorial flourish.  
- **Body line length caps around 70 characters** on desktop for readability.  
- **Two or three weights per piece**, no more. The family gives you many; using many is how a layout starts to look nervous.

### Campaign and story accent fonts

For gamer-facing campaign art only, never the website, three display faces set atmosphere. Choose by the feeling you want:

- **Lazydog** for cartoonish, playful, speech-bubble contexts.  
- **Shlop** for spooky content (Halloween camps and the like).  
- **True Typewriter** for mystery and adventure atmosphere.

These are mood tools for posters and social, chosen deliberately per campaign. They never appear in parent-facing, safety, or informational copy.

### The two faces you cannot set yourself

The logo's own lettering is locked artwork, not type you can reuse:

- **The "SOG" monogram** in the logo is a custom gamified display face. It exists only inside the logo. Do not recreate it for headlines or hunt for a lookalike; its scarcity is what makes the badge feel special.  
- **The condensed sans** in "SCHOOL OF GAMING" likewise lives only in the lockup.

If a headline ever needs more energy than Poppins and the campaign faces provide, that is a brief for a designer, not a free gaming font grabbed off the internet.

Now that the logo is a registered EUTM, the ® symbol is available and its use signals a defended asset.

- **Territory matters.** The registration is EU-wide. Outside the EU the mark may not be registered, and ® should not be claimed where it is not. When in doubt in a non-EU market, ask the representative before using ®.  
- **In running body copy**, writing "School of Gaming" as words needs no symbol. The ® attaches to the logo mark, not to every mention of the name in a sentence.

Until that registration completes:

- **Keep using Gedu exactly as this guide already specifies.** Consistent, capitalized, never generic. Consistent use strengthens the application; sloppy use weakens it.  
- **Do not attach ® to "Gedu"** yet, because it is not registered. Do not attach ™ either without checking with the representative, since a premature or incorrect symbol can complicate a pending filing.

# Appendix B. Campaign toolkit and photography

This appendix holds the campaign and social visual system reconciled from the Notion brand book: photography rules, the recurring visual elements, the social post templates, and the basic UI. It is the design-execution layer that sits under the identity in Appendix A. Where a designer's dedicated system disagrees with this, the designer's system wins; this is the shared reference so writers and content builders start from the same place.

## B.1 Photography and imagery

**Real photography is the default. AI imagery is a fallback, not a first choice.**

The reason is authenticity. Real photos of real children in real sessions communicate what a generated image cannot, and for a brand whose entire promise is safety and genuineness, the imagery has to be as honest as the copy. So: use real photography wherever it exists. When a suitable real image genuinely is not available, an AI-generated image is permitted rather than shipping something worse or blank. Treat AI as the understudy, and never let it play a role a real photo should.

**The absolute rule, which AI does not soften: never show children's faces.** This holds for every image, real or generated, everywhere, social, website, webinars, print, decks, with no exception and regardless of whether parental permission exists. It is itself a brand statement: it communicates child safety before a single word is read.

How to shoot children:

- From behind or over the shoulder, hands on keyboards, screens visible, backs to camera.  
- Or faces blurred in post before anything publishes.  
- Focus on hands, builds on screen, the moment rather than the face.

**Whose faces are welcome:** Gedus and team members. Their faces build trust and are a feature, not a risk. Real parent reactions (watching, smiling, reading session notes) are welcome too, as adults.

**Priority subjects:** children's hands on keyboards from behind; a child's back or shoulder facing a Minecraft build; Gedus' faces and hands guiding players; real parent reactions; the team at work; the Lauttasaari flagship space; Sogverse screenshots inside the window frame (see B.2).

**Photo library discipline:** tag every new photo by Yty-Element and content type so the right image is findable when a template needs it.

## B.2 Recurring visual elements

These are the repeating devices that make a post recognisably School of Gaming. Use them for what they are for; do not sprinkle them for decoration.

**The SOG Blobs (in-house name: kätyrit).** Our original mascot characters: small, round, black bodies, one eye, little legs. They are always "Blobs". **Never call them "minions" in any context, public or internal**, that word is Universal Pictures IP and using it is a legal exposure we simply avoid. Known variants: default (plain black, neutral), Energy (violet hat, excited, for Big News), Creative (holding a paintbrush, for making moments), Community (pink hat, softer, for game-screenshot posts). Rules: always pair a Blob with the logo, never as the only brand element; never use them where a human face belongs (testimonials, staff profiles).

**The game-screenshot window frame.** A retro browser-style window (blue title bar, X button top left) frames every in-game screenshot, creating the feeling of peeking into Sogverse. Always frame in-game content this way; never post a raw screenshot.

**Sticky-tape / scrapbook element.** Creamy diagonal tape strips that "pin" white cards to coloured backgrounds, for a handmade feel. Use on testimonial and quote cards. Do not use on the marketing website, social and campaigns only.

**Scribble / doodle elements.** Hand-drawn white hearts, arrows, circles, exclamation marks laid over photos to add warmth. Use on photo posts and team/personal moments. Do not use on informational or safety content, warmth is wrong there.

**Colour background blocks for text.** Rounded rectangles in a Yty or Energy colour, either solid colour with white text or a white card with ink text. Never pick a block colour that clashes with the photo behind it.

**Topic tag / category pill.** A small rounded pill in a Yty or amber colour, label in Poppins bold caps (BLOG, TOPIC), always top-left of the text area.

**Speech bubbles.** Two styles: a rounded white cartoon bubble for in-game characters speaking, and a large Poppins-black text headline for brand claims and opinions.

**"OMG\!" scribble label.** A handwritten "OMG\!" for genuinely exciting Big News only, upper left. Reserved, not routine, or it stops meaning anything.

## B.3 Social post templates

Six formats. Each has colour variants, choose the variant matching the Yty-Element the content serves (B.2 and Appendix A.2).

1. **Tagline over photo.** Full-bleed real photo, logo centred top, colour text box at bottom with a bold tagline, topic tag above. For awareness and scene-setting. Copy: 1-2 line tagline, left-aligned.  
2. **Highlights box.** Photo with a large rounded rectangle of bullet points. For tips, lists, how-to, parent guides. Copy: bold intro line, 3-5 bullets, 40 words max in the box.  
3. **Testimonial card.** Coloured background, white scrapbook card with sticky tape, bold quote, attribution in script. For social proof. Copy: real quotes only, attributed "First name, Country".  
4. **Game screenshot.** Coloured background, in-game screenshot in the window frame, caption below, optional Blob. For showcasing clubs and builds. Copy: describe what is happening in the world.  
5. **Bold claim / opinion.** Dark blurred photo, large speech bubble with white text. For taking a stance. Copy: short, punchy, may end on a question.  
6. **Big News.** Bright solid colour background, "BIG NEWS" in Poppins black, the OMG\! scribble, tagline, amber CTA button, Energy-hat Blob. For launches and major milestones only. Copy: short tagline, single amber CTA.

## Consequences worth noticing

Three of these rulings have effects beyond the line item, and they are the ones most likely to cause trouble if they go unread.

**Sogo has no defined rate, and that is a constraint on the store, not a detail.** Until a rate exists, store copy may say what Sogo buys but must never imply how it is earned or at what speed. "340 Sogo" is fine. "Just three lessons away" is not, because we cannot currently make that true. Section 8.6 has been written to hold under this constraint.

**Section 10.10 now contains an aspirational example.** The Tuomas post describes a former gamer entering Level 1 Pathfinder. No gamer has reached the Gedu Path yet, so **that post must not be published as written.** It stays in the guide as a model of how the story should be told on the day it becomes true. Until then, the loop is described as a path that exists and is open, never as one people are already walking.

**Human Skills gives Level 1 copy something it was missing.** Until now, plain-language copy to parents and institutions had no vocabulary of its own: it could only describe the Yty-Elements from outside, or fall back on borrowed education-sector language. Human Skills is the translated register in its own right, and the seven map cleanly onto the four. Section 5.2 carries the mapping. Expect partner, press, and website copy to change more than anything else as a result.

**"Prices vary" is now a formatting rule, not a disclaimer.** Because Gedus will set their own prices, any single figure in marketing becomes a promise we cannot keep at checkout. "From €59 per month" is the standing construction.

## Live-site drift, worth fixing at the source

Four inconsistencies between the published sites and this guide. None are urgent; all are cheap; each costs a little credibility where it sits.

| Where | What it says | What it should say |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| sogverse.sog.gg, About | "— The Principal of the School of Gaming" | "— The Princi-Pal" |
| sogverse.sog.gg, Make Gaming a Great Hobby | "a professional instructor who is there to guide and help" | "a Gedu who is there to guide and help" |
| sogverse.sog.gg, throughout | "clubs" used for both the group and the session | "club" for the group, "lesson" for the 90 minute session |
| sogverse.sog.gg, throughout | Minecraft presented as the whole offer | Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite, and Rocket League, with the games framed as dimensions rather than the definition |

The last one is the most costly. The site currently reads as a Minecraft club business, which undersells a multi-game platform and will need rewriting again every time a title is added. Framing the games as dimensions of Sogverse makes the copy survive the next addition.
