# Products Architecture

The `products` domain: four product types (consumer clubs, municipality clubs, camps, events) as **one unified shape**, priced in EUR. In production.

**This doc is the high-level map, not the reference.** It carries the mental model and the rules an agent can't infer from code, plus where to look. Concrete shapes and behaviour live in code and are the source of truth — don't restate them here, they rot:

- **Schema** (columns, constraints, enums, RPC signatures and bodies): `supabase/schema.sql` + `src/types/database.types.ts`.
- **Behaviour**: the services and components in the "Where to look" map below.

Adjacent systems have their own colocated docs: `src/services/locations/CLAUDE.md`, `src/components/voice/CLAUDE.md`, `src/lib/email-templates/CLAUDE.md`, `src/services/whatsapp/CLAUDE.md`, `src/components/session-feed/CLAUDE.md`, `src/components/family/product-page/CLAUDE.md`. Stripe test cards, the refund and VAT-audit procedures, and the webhook deployment runbook: `docs/runbooks/stripe.md`.

## The model

| | Consumer club | Municipality club | Camp | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parent verb | Enroll | Register | Sign up | Join |
| Who pays | Parent monthly, or free | Municipality, off-platform | Parent upfront, or free | Parent upfront, or free |
| `billing_mode` | `paid` or `free` | `external_contract` | `paid` or `free` | `paid` or `free` |
| Schedule | Recurring, open-ended | Recurring, term-bounded | Recurring, camp-bounded | One-off |
| Capacity | Optional cap | Cap required | Optional cap | Optional cap |
| Discovery | `/shop` | `/schools` | `/shop` | `/shop` |

The four share ~80% of the operational model (schedule, location, topic, language, audience and — where there is a gamer audience — an age range, gedus, participation, attendance, notes, waitlist, and, online only, a voice room). They differ on **pricing shape** and **schedule shape**, captured as small orthogonal fields rather than separate tables.

**A seat cap is one field on every type, and the money decides how it binds:** hard on a no-charge signup (it cannot be oversold), soft on a paid one (see §Seat gate). A waitlist is available behind a cap on any type. Municipality clubs are the one type where a cap is mandatory rather than optional.

**Rule: branch on the orthogonal fields (`billing_mode`, schedule shape, `seat_count`), never on `product_type`.** `product_type` is a label for UI and filtering only. (This is what lets a future on-platform muni billing mode slot in without per-type switches everywhere.)

## Naming & vocabulary

- **"Municipality club," never "school club"** (schema `municipality_club`). They run at libraries, community centres, school rooms — anchoring the name to "school" is inaccurate. Don't introduce "school club" in copy, URLs, schema, or docs.
- **Parent-facing verbs differ per type** (Enroll / Register / Sign up / Join). Copy only — they never reach schema, query keys, RPC names, or URLs.
- **One schema noun: `participations`** — a **participant's** seat (or waitlist spot) on a product, any type. A seat holds whichever profile occupies it: a child's, or the buying parent's own, in which case participant = customer. There is deliberately no second table and no seat-kind column — who is in the seat is derivable from the row, and a second noun would fork every predicate, roster and counter (see §Audience).
- **"Participant" in the schema, the person's own word in the copy.** The column, the RPC parameters and the wire fields all say participant; parent-facing copy says the child's name, or the second person when the reader is the participant. A family never meets the word.
- **Audience labels are two words, and the third shape has none:** a parents-only product is "For parents", a both-audiences one is "For families", and a gamers-only one — what every product was — wears nothing. Those two labels are the browse chips as well as the badges (see §Audience).
- **Participation state is derived from the row, never stored:** `waitlisted` (status), `unassigned` (active + no group), `assigned` (active + group). **All three are distinct to a family**, and `unassigned` is the one that is easy to get wrong: a seat that is bought and paid for but not yet placed has no group, so it has no feed and no page, and it must offer nothing to open and no room to join. It reads as awaiting a gedu match, which is what it is.

## Core invariants

The rules to honour; the *enforcement* lives in the SQL/code.

### Seat gate & the create-on-payment rule

- **The product-row lock is the signup gate.** Every participation mutation begins `SELECT 1 FROM products WHERE id = $1 FOR UPDATE`, so concurrent create/cancel/promote on one product serialize and seat math is race-free. **Never `SKIP LOCKED` / `NOWAIT`** — callers must wait to see consistent post-commit state.
- **A paid participation is created when the money arrives, never before.** The pre-Checkout call under the lock *validates* the signup (audience and self-vs-parent-link first — see §Audience — then lifecycle, registration window, currency, already-enrolled, seat cap) and writes nothing; the Stripe `checkout.session.completed` webhook creates the `active` row. An abandoned, expired or failed checkout therefore leaves nothing in the database, and no path has a seat to release by hand. The no-charge shapes (any free product, municipality registration) still activate in the same request as the validation, because there is nothing to wait for.
- **One number holds a seat: `status = 'active'`.** The capacity gate, the rollup the parent-facing counter reads, and the seat-left pill all count that and only that. They used to disagree — a pre-payment hold counted in one and not the other — and the gap turned a stranded hold into a permanently destroyed seat.
- **A paid participation records the Checkout Session that bought it.** That single column carries two loads: it lets the confirmation page resolve a Stripe `success_url` back to a seat, and it lets a redelivered webhook tell its own earlier work from a genuine second payment. Without it the two are indistinguishable, and the retry path — the handler writes its payment row last on purpose, so a failed write leaves no commit marker and Stripe re-runs it — would read its own row as a duplicate charge and cancel a paying customer's live subscription.
- **A second payment for one (product, participant) is answered, not stored.** The confirmation RPC returns a duplicate verdict naming the row that is already there; the webhook records the charge for a manual refund and, for a subscription, cancels it at Stripe — refunding one invoice would not stop the next one.
- **The confirmation page never waits for something that isn't coming.** Creating the seat after the redirect means the page can arrive before it exists, so it waits — but it tells "not written *yet*" apart from "this payment bought nothing, because the seat was already taken", and it bounds the wait either way. A spinner under "this only takes a moment" is a promise; a page with no order coming has to stop and say where to look instead. Refunds are manual, so no state may promise one.
- **A cap is a hard cap on no-charge signups and a soft cap on paid ones. That duality is the policy, and it is why no pre-payment hold came back.** A no-charge shape (free product, municipality registration) validates the cap and writes its `active` row inside the same locked transaction, so it cannot be oversold — the cap is absolute. A paid shape is gated before Checkout, so once a product is full a new entrant is refused there; but the webhook that confirms the payment **deliberately never re-checks**, because a refusal after the money has moved is worse than one visible seat over the line and refunds are manual. Everyone who passed the gate and completed Checkout therefore keeps their seat, including the one that takes the product over its cap. **Overfull is a legitimate state:** anything computing seats-left for a family clamps at zero, and the admin panel shows the overfill rather than clamping it away.
- **The overselling this accepts is bounded by concurrent Checkout sessions on a nearly-full product** — a Stripe session lives about half an hour, so the exposure is however many parents are sitting on the payment page for the last seats at once. At today's volume (hundreds of profiles) that is realistically one or two seats, resolved by an admin who can see the overfill. **This is a volume-dependent assumption, not a mechanism guarantee** — if a genuinely hot product launch ever becomes a thing, revisit it before relying on it. What it replaces is the pre-payment hold, which died of stranded rows (written before the Checkout Session existed, so nothing could expire them) and of two seat counts that disagreed; reintroducing one is not the answer to a capped paid product, and the create-on-payment rules above are the record of what removing it cost.
- **The admin form gates none of this by type.** Seat caps and waitlists are available on all four types. What the form still keys to the money is the *default*, so that forgetting to cap is not a thing that happens: a free product defaults to capped with a required number, a paid one defaults uncapped, and a municipality club must carry a cap outright (see §Admin surfaces).
- **Promoting a never-paid family onto a *subscription-shaped* product is blocked by an explanatory admin dialog, and `promote_from_waitlist` carries no billing guard of its own — that is a decided posture, not an outstanding hazard.** Promotion writes an `active` seat with no payment step, which on a product whose seat cannot exist without a recurring charge would hand out a free one indefinitely. **The condition is narrower than "the product costs money", and deliberately so.** It fires for a paid consumer club — the one shape billed as a subscription, and the same predicate the admin-enrolment refusal uses — **and** only when the waitlisted row carries no payment marker (the Checkout Session it recorded). A paid camp or paid event is settled by a single payment the admin handles out of band, so a plain drag there is trusted and goes straight through; keying the dialog to billing mode alone would have put a confirmation in front of the commonest legitimate promotion there is. The marker half matters because demotion preserves it: a family who genuinely paid and was later moved to the queue promotes back with a plain drag, while a family who only ever queued gets the dialog. Without it, every demoted paying member would be one-way-trapped on the queue. The manual path the dialog names is the front door — the seat that just opened shows the product as open, so the family leaves the waitlist from their My SOG card and buys through normal checkout, where every existing race is already handled. The RPC stays unguarded under the same admin-trust model the form locks rest on: admins act through the UI, and the dialog is the guard. If admins meet that dialog often, that is the signal to build a real promotion-payment flow.
- **No client-supplied prices.** The client sends `(product_id, participant_id, purchase_shape, currency)`; the server recomputes the charge from the stored base price every time.

### Audience — who a product admits, and where that is checked

- **Two independent booleans on `products` decide it: `for_gamers` and `for_parents`, with a CHECK that at least one is true.** Both are available on **all four types** — a parents' evening and a family outing are events, a club a parent attends alongside their child is a club, and the model deliberately never decides which of those may exist. Everything authored before the flags is gamers-only and stays so (the defaults are true / false).
- **A parent's own seat is the row where participant = customer, and nothing downstream cares.** The old CHECK forbidding it is gone; its *protective* intent — a payer can't casually occupy a child's seat — survives as the gates below. `(product_id, participant)` uniqueness, seat counting (**a parent's seat consumes a seat like any other**), waitlist ordering, the checkout-session column, the free/external immediate-insert paths, attendance (a gedu marks a parent present exactly like a gamer) and the participation-keyed family product page all read the seat without asking who is in it.
- **Ages belong to the gamer audience and to nothing else.** `min_age`/`max_age` are non-null **iff** `for_gamers`, by CHECK: a product with a gamer audience must carry both bounds, one without carries neither. Cards and detail pages render an age line only when a range exists.
- **Rule: never express an adult audience as an age range.** "18+", 0–99 and every other sentinel was rejected outright — age is the wrong vocabulary for "this is for parents" (it reads as *any adult gamer welcome*) and it poisons the age data for everything that reads it. The audience flags carry that meaning, and the CHECK is what stops the two being used interchangeably.
- **The shop's age band asks "what age is the child I am shopping for", so a range-less product drops out of a banded search by construction** rather than by a rule of its own — surfacing parents-only products under a child's age is noise. The audience chips are the tool for shopping for yourself; that is why the two rows never fold into one.
- **A chip is the badge.** Each chip matches exactly the products wearing that label, so lighting both chips is *narrower* than lighting none (gamers-only answers neither), and clearing the row is the only way back to everything. Collapsing the two booleans into the three-shape vocabulary happens in one module, which is what keeps badge, chip and style guide from drifting.
- Age remains advisory — it blocks nothing — and the door to enforcing it later is *cleaner* for this: any future check runs on gamer seats against the gamer's date of birth, and parent seats are structurally exempt rather than special-cased.
- **Staff surfaces render an adult participant as its own row variant**: an adult badge, the participant's **own** email as contact (there is no linked parent to borrow one from — the roster reads emit the field), and the child-shaped lines — age, gender, the game account — **omitted outright**, not rendered as empty placeholders. A shorter row is the design: an "unlinked" slot or a blank cell would be an affordance pointing at nothing, since game-account linking for adults is not built and adults carry no roster age. Reserve nothing for content that can never arrive.

**The gates live before the money, and only there.**

- **Every pre-money enrollment path checks the audience in both directions.** Participant = customer (a self seat) requires `for_parents`. Participant ≠ customer requires the `parent_gamer` link (unchanged) **and** `for_gamers`, so a parents-only product refuses a child seat and an unlinked adult is refused exactly as an unlinked child is — which is what keeps *a parent can never enroll another adult* true. Three functions carry it: `create_participation`, `join_waitlist`, and `admin_enroll_participant`. **`join_waitlist` is the single gate point for queueing** — its public wrapper only asserts the caller's role and pins the customer to the session user, so there is exactly one body to change and no second copy to forget. Leaving a queue needs no audience thought at all, being authorized on the customer.
- **"Self" is expressible only as participant = the customer the caller passed.** The participation writers are service-role-only and have no `auth.uid()`; the route pins the customer to the session user, which is the same footing the parent-link check has always stood on. The comparison is a plain `=` and not `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM`, deliberately: two NULL ids are not a self seat but a caller with nothing to say, and the NULL comparison drops them into the parent-link arm that refuses them — so fail-closed falls out of the operator rather than out of a guard somewhere above it.
- **`confirm_paid_participation` carries no audience gate. That is the decision, not an omission.** It is the after-money recorder: service-role-only, reachable only through the signature-verified Stripe webhook, fed metadata our own server wrote *after* validation passed. A gate there defends against no attacker — anyone who could feed it an unvalidated tuple already holds the webhook secret or the server — and it manufactures a real failure with money in it: an admin unticking an audience flag between checkout and webhook would make the webhook refuse **forever**, the charge stands, no seat exists, and refunds are manual. The accepted cost is the other side of that same race: a seat created on a product whose audience changed seconds earlier, which is a stale-but-visible enrollment an admin handles like any other roster question. **Validate before money; trust and record after** — the same posture the seat cap already takes, and for the same reason.
- **`promote_from_waitlist` is a fourth path to an active seat, and it too carries no audience gate — accepted, not overlooked.** An admin dragging a waitlisted row to a seat writes `active` under `assert_admin` + the product lock, without re-reading the audience. The only way it produces an audience-mismatched seat is the admin *first* changing a product's audience out from under people already in its queue (an audience edit does not purge the queue — only turning the waitlist itself off does), then promoting one of them. That sequence is not a workflow anyone has reason to run, and gating here would strand a queued family behind an admin action with no money and no expiry on the other side of it — the confirm-paid rationale does not transfer, because there is no charge to protect. **The decision is to accept it, and the invariant that makes it safe is that audience gates entry, never rendering:** an audience-mismatched active seat — a gamer in a since-parents-only group, or a parent in a since-gamers-only group — renders correctly everywhere (rollup, roster, voice, attendance all read the seat without asking the product's audience), so the worst case is a roster oddity an admin resolves by hand, exactly like an over-cap seat. Do not "fix" this by gating promotion without first reckoning with the stranded-queue cost.

### Tags — who a product was designed for

- **One optional tag per product, from a fixed enum of exactly three values (`neuroinclusive`, `beginner`, `advanced`), on a nullable `tag` column.** It answers a different question from the audience beside it: an audience says who may *hold a seat*, a tag says who the thing was *built for*. A club for children who have never played, one for advanced builders, and one designed around neurodivergent kids are otherwise indistinguishable on a grid behind an age range.
- **The single nullable column *is* the design, not a shortcut.** Multiple tags and a join table were rejected by the product owner for v1: the card has one chip slot and "Beginner + Advanced" is incoherent. **If a second axis ever appears** — skill level versus support need, the fault line running through the current three — **it arrives as a second column, not as N tags.**
- **Null is untagged, and untagged renders nothing.** No chip on the browse card, no chip on the detail hero, no explanation block under the short description. This is the same shape a gamers-only audience has, which renders no badge: the absence of a label is the ordinary state and must never become an empty placeholder. There is no default and no backfill — every product authored before the column existed is untagged because nobody has said otherwise.
- **A chip is the tag.** A chip on the shop's tag filter row matches exactly the products wearing that tag; selected chips combine with OR; and untagged products answer only an empty row — so lighting every chip is *narrower* than lighting none, and clearing the row is the only way back to the untagged majority. The rule is recorded here rather than with the filter because it constrains the *badge* just as much: badge and chip read the same resolution, so the two cannot drift.
- **The tag filter row copies the audience row's pattern rather than inventing one** — the same chip treatment and multi-select behaviour, the same URL persistence (one comma-separated param, read through a guard so an unknown or renamed value resolves to no selection instead of emptying the grid), and the same labels the cards wear, resolved through the tag module's key map rather than spelled from the enum. It sits **directly under the audience row**, the two being the neighbouring halves of one question — who may hold a seat, and who the sessions were built for. Unlike the audience row it **also renders on the per-municipality school pages** (owner decision, Aug 2026): Type and Audience hide there because every product on a school page is that school's own gamers-only club and both rows would have one answer, but a tag is orthogonal to that structure — one school can offer a beginner club beside a neuroinclusive one, and choosing between a school's own clubs is that page's whole question.
- **The filter chips carry the tag glyphs.** The tag vocabulary is icon-and-word wherever it is met — the browse card, the detail hero, the admin picker that set it — so the filter is the wrong place to show half of it, and the glyph comes from the same shared map the chips do rather than from a second one beside it.
- **The ordered value list and the string guard live in the tag module**, derived from the generated enum and never hand-written, and both the filter row and the admin picker enumerate from that one list — so the two offer the same vocabulary in the same order, and a value added by migration reaches both the moment types are regenerated.
- **The row shipped in Aug 2026 on engineering's call, ahead of its original trigger, and the design is the one that was settled while it was deferred.** The gate was the product owner's go-ahead — a filter earns its place only once the tagged catalogue is wide enough to need narrowing — not engineering readiness; it was greenlit before the catalogue reached that width, and nothing about the semantics above was reopened in the process.
- **Corner exclusivity on the card: the top-right chip is the audience badge when there is one, otherwise the age range — never both.** The detail hero resolves the pair the same way, so a card and the page it opens can never show different halves of it. The tag is the *other* corner and is not part of that contest.
- **The neuroinclusive tag is never illustrated with a puzzle piece — under any circumstance.** It is a contested symbol in the neurodivergent community and is not ours to reclaim on a shop card. The icon map carries a comment saying so; it is not a stylistic preference to be tidied away by a later reader.
- **"Neuroinclusive", never "neurodivergent-friendly".** The "-friendly" suffix implies every unlabelled club is unfriendly; the chosen word states a design property without ranking the rest of the catalogue. The same reasoning drove the Finnish label. Re-ruling a label is a one-string change in the message files and nothing structural.
- **Both product writers carry the tag, and its parameter is *defaulted* where the audience flags are not.** Null is a legal value and no CHECK backstops it, so the audience's "make omission fail loudly" trick is unavailable — and a non-defaulted parameter would be worse than useless, because the generated argument types make a required argument non-nullable, leaving no way to express "clear this" at all. The risk is closed one step earlier instead: **the create and update body schemas both make the field required-nullable**, so a caller cannot omit it by accident, and the route maps an explicit null to an omitted argument, which the default turns into a cleared column. That chain — null on the wire, absent in the call, NULL in the row — is the intended meaning of clearing a tag (see §Known gaps for the general rule this is the deliberate exception to).
- **The tag is a product fact, not a permission.** It gates nothing: no signup path reads it, no RLS predicate mentions it, and an admin may change it freely for the product's whole life. It is not part of the form-lock machinery for the same reason.

### Region lock — where a product is sold

- **One optional country per product, on a nullable ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 column; NULL is unlocked and is the ordinary state.** It answers yet another question from the two above: an audience says who may hold a seat, a tag says who the sessions were built for, and the lock says which country the thing is *sold in*. The admin picker offers the seeded supported countries and nothing else, so a lock always names a country the location tree can actually resolve a family into.
- **It compares the product's country against the family's own, which is a self-attested profile field**: the country of the `locations` row a parent's home location points at. A parent who has never set one has no country, and that is a distinct state from having the wrong one.
- **Four states, three of which the signup panel says something about.** An **unlocked** product is untouched — no trace that anything was checked, because nothing was. A family with **no location** keeps the whole signup form and gains a section between the participant picker and the rules, asking where they live and carrying the action that answers it; the CTA names that step and stays disabled, because the panel's grammar is that the CTA submits and the sections act. An **eligible** family — locked product, matching country — keeps the form too and gets that same section in the same place, stating where the product is offered and where the family lives. A family in the **wrong country** loses the form entirely to a block naming the country: there is no decision for that reader, so none is presented.
- **The refusal is louder than the wrong-role note it sits beside, and toned as information.** They look like the same thing and are met by different readers: a gedu or a gamer on a shop URL already knows they are not the audience, so a muted line only confirms it, while a parent who came to buy does not know — and an inert panel with a small grey line under it reads as a page that failed to load. So the refusal is a first-class tinted block at body size, with the country carrying visible weight because it is the one fact the reader is scanning for. It is toned **info**, never warning or error (nothing has gone wrong and nothing is theirs to fix) and never the panel's action colour (there is nothing to act on).
- **All three surfaces the lock speaks through share the info hue, at two volumes, and reading as one family is the point.** The refusal, the no-location question and the eligible confirmation are one subject told at three moments — this product is a bit different, it wants your attention, nothing is wrong — so each carries the info-coloured border, an info-coloured glyph and its own words at full foreground weight (a receipt line beneath may stay muted). A parent who is asked for a location and then told it fits should hear the second as the same voice as the first. Volume follows stakes: the refusal replaces the whole panel and is the one thing on it, so it alone fills its surface with the info tint and weights the country inside its sentence; the two in-form sections sit inside a form the parent is actively using, so they carry the hue on the border alone — the same quiet-info tier the enrollment card's awaiting state established — and the form stays the loudest thing on its own panel.
- **The eligible state is a statement, never an affordance.** It carries no button and adds no step to the CTA's checklist: an eligible family has nothing left to do, and inviting a location edit halfway through a purchase would be inviting the one change that could break it. Settings owns changing a location. What the state buys is the two things a silent pass could not: the ask-section becomes its own answer in place when a matching location is confirmed, which is how the parent learns the save worked, and the sentence about where a product is offered is worth making to the people it is true for as well as to those it is not.
- **The wrong-country copy names the country and the no-location copy does not.** A refusal that will not say what it is refusing on is useless to the reader, while naming the country in the *question* would turn it into a hint about which answer unlocks the page. The eligible copy names it too, for the same reason the refusal does — and adds the family's own location beneath, because a parent who has just answered the question wants to see which answer was recorded.
- **The refusal and the eligible line are two speech acts, and therefore two strings.** The conditional wording that is exactly right for the excluded reader — the product is *only* offered somewhere else — reads as a hedge to the reader who is already in, who is told instead that the product is *just* for families here — a short, warm fragment that leads with the special-ness and lands on belonging, leaving the receipt line beneath to do the "that's you" work in their own place name. Same underlying fact, opposite direction, and nothing should ever pin the two to each other.
- **Both name the country in label position — after a colon, at the end of the sentence — and no locale's copy may inflect it.** The name comes from the platform's own locale data, which supplies a bare nominative and nothing else: dropped into a sentence it needs an article in English ("families in *the* United Kingdom") and a case ending in Finnish, and there is no correct constant to hardcode because the answer differs per country. As a label it is grammatical for every country there is. So each string asks its question, then names the country, and the constraint binds every translation rather than only the English.
- **A read that fails, a location row carrying no country, or a confirmed pick carrying no country, all fail open.** The gate is a soft signal with nothing behind it, so the two errors are not symmetric: blocking an honest buyer — or standing them in front of a question they have already answered — costs a sale over a fault of ours, while letting one family past an unenforced lock costs nothing the design was relying on. All three resolve to the unlocked state rather than to the question, which also keeps the panel from offering a dialog that would overwrite a location already on file — and keeps the one path that answers the question from ending by asking it again.
- **A fail-open is latched for the life of the page's mount, never re-decided per render.** The read behind the gate retries and refetches on its own schedule, so a page that sampled it would paint the full form on a failed read and then swap it for a refusal when a later attempt succeeded — destroying a picker selection and a ticked consent, on data's schedule rather than the reader's. The first failed attempt is therefore remembered: once a visit has resolved the gate without the read, a late answer cannot rewrite it. For the same reason the page stops *waiting* on that read at its first failure rather than at the end of its retry window — the wait and the fail-open have to give up at the same moment, or the page paints on the strength of a read it is still listening to.
- **The block is UI-only, and nothing behind it enforces anything.** No RLS predicate, no RPC check, no route guard reads the column; a family that reaches the participation RPC by other means is not stopped. That is the accepted design rather than an unfinished one: the input being compared is a field the blocked party can rewrite in their own settings, so a server-side gate would be enforcing an assertion the assertee controls. The bypass is therefore real, known and accepted — and never advertised, which is why no string anywhere mentions that changing the setting walks straight through it.
- **A locked product is still browsed, listed, linked and read by anybody.** Nothing filters the storefront, the school pages or search on it; the lock is a statement made on the signup panel, at the moment of purchase, and nowhere else.
- **Enrolment is checked once, at signup, and never revisited.** A family that moves afterwards keeps its seat, its sessions and its subscription, and a lock added to a product later does not touch anybody already on it. Nothing sweeps existing participations against the column.
- **A family that moves to a non-matching country still meets the full wrong-country overlay on the shop page, seat or no seat, and that is deliberate.** The overlay replaces the whole panel including the already-joined row, so nothing there acknowledges what they hold — but nothing takes it away either: what they already own stays on their own dashboard, on the family product page keyed by the participation, which is the surface for that. What they lose is buying *further* seats until their location matches again, which is the gate gating entrance, exactly as it does for everybody else.

### Billing

- **EUR-only authoring + Stripe Adaptive Pricing at checkout.** Authored, displayed, and recorded in EUR; Stripe presents the customer their local currency and settles us in EUR. The currency columns/CHECKs stay dormant for a future multi-currency re-enable — single seam is `SUPPORTED_CURRENCIES`.
- **One purchase option per product, decided by the product** (not a menu): paid consumer club → monthly subscription; paid camp / paid event → single upfront; any free product, club, camp or event → no checkout; muni → the `external` shape (instant `active`, invoiced off-platform, no Stripe). `free` and `external` are the two no-charge shapes, each gated on its matching `billing_mode`.
- **"Charges nothing" is a named set on both sides of the wire, because the colloquial "free" means both no-charge modes.** `billing_mode` has three members and they are not three equal alternatives: `paid` is one thing, and `free` and `external_contract` are two ways of being the other thing — and when anyone here says a product is free they nearly always mean the pair, a genuinely free product *and* a municipality club (currently the only consumer of `external_contract`). So the two-versus-paid question has one name in each language — `NO_CHARGE_BILLING_MODES` / `isNoChargeBillingMode` in TypeScript, `public.is_no_charge(billing_mode)` in Postgres — and they move in lockstep; widening one without the other is how the UI and the database start disagreeing. **The distinction between the two modes stays load-bearing**, which is why this is a named set rather than a collapsed enum: each mode gates its own purchase shape, so wherever the answer differs per mode the explicit comparison is the correct code and the set is the wrong tool.
- **One Stripe subscription per (participant, club)** — one `family_subscriptions` row per participation (the "family" name is historical; a row is one person's seat in one club, and that person is usually a child but may be the paying parent). Buys: always-Checkout (the trust moment), per-club cancel via Stripe's portal, and deferred billing (below).
- **A club starting more than an hour from now defers its first charge to its start date, and the deferral is a Checkout parameter rather than a state we keep.** The session sets a billing anchor at product-local midnight on `start_date` with prorations disabled, so the parent pays **€0 at checkout** while the subscription is `active` from that moment. The one-hour floor exists because the anchor is computed when the session is created but the subscription only when the buyer completes it, up to half an hour later — an anchor already in the past at completion would be refused, so a club that close to starting simply charges immediately, as a started one does — the seat is held and the sessions show, exactly as for any other member. No trial is involved anywhere, deliberately: a trial reads as "free for a while" and Stripe frames it that way to the buyer, which is the wrong sentence for a club that simply has not begun. Three consequences worth knowing before touching this. **The anchor is clamped** to roughly four weeks out, because Stripe refuses one later than the buyer's next natural billing date — so a parent who buys two months early is charged before the club starts, which is an accepted product decision and must be *stated honestly* wherever a first-charge date is shown (showing the start date there would be a lie about money). **A €0 completion arrives as `no_payment_required`, not `paid`**, so any gate that asks "did this session buy something" has to accept both, for subscription-mode sessions only — a one-off that collected nothing bought nothing. And **the first real charge arrives as an ordinary `subscription_cycle` renewal** weeks later, preceded in our ledger by a zero-amount payment row that exists as the webhook's idempotency marker. Whether Stripe also raises a €0 invoice at creation is deliberately not relied on either way: anything born with the subscription carries `billing_reason: "subscription_create"` and the renewal recorder skips it.
- **The anchor is stamped once and never revisited.** An admin who moves a product's start date afterwards does not move the first-charge date of subscriptions already sold; that correction is manual in the Stripe dashboard, the admin start-date field says so, and the automated sync is a recorded follow-up in `TODO.md`.
- **Club cancellation is portal-only.** No in-app cancel action. Parent cancels in Stripe's hosted portal → `customer.subscription.deleted` webhook → `cancel_participation` hard-deletes the participation (CASCADEs the sub row). Webhook-driven and idempotent; the app never calls Stripe back on that path.
- **VAT treatment is a product fact, derived from `product_type` and stored nowhere.** Camps are sold under Finland's reduced-rate Stripe tax category (13.5% today); consumer clubs and paid events under the standard one (25.5% today). Municipality clubs map to standard defensively and never reach Stripe at all, being `external_contract`. That camps qualify for the reduced rate, that a particular Stripe category is the right one for them, and that paid events are standard-rated are all **finance rulings by the CFO**, recorded rather than inferred — revisiting any of them is a finance decision, not an implementation one. There is no column, no RPC parameter and no migration behind this: the treatment is re-derived every time it is needed.
- **One mapping module in `src/lib/stripe/` is the only place a category or a rate is written down**, imported by the checkout path, the admin form and the backfill script under `scripts/` alike, so a future ruling change is one edit. **No percentage is persisted or computed with** — Stripe resolves the real rate from the tax category, the customer's location and the date of sale. The percentage in that module exists only to be displayed, and it goes stale the next time Finland moves a band (the reduced rate fell from 14% to 13.5% on 1 January 2026); a stale number there misinforms an admin reading a form, it cannot mischarge a customer.
- **Admins see the treatment and cannot set it.** The product form shows the treatment and the rate it resolves to, read-only, and only on a **paid** product — a free or municipality product issues no Stripe sale, and a percentage shown there would read as a claim about an invoice we never send. An editable treatment was considered and rejected: unconstrained choice is how a wrong rate gets picked. `product_type` has no UI or RPC path that changes it after creation, which is what makes a derived treatment stable in practice; it is not a database guarantee, but a later purchase re-derives from whatever the row says.
- **Every product that reaches Stripe gets a shared Stripe product carrying that tax category**, created lazily and reused thereafter — camps and events included, where the line item used to be an inline product with no category and so fell through to the account-wide default. One-off line items keep an inline *price* (the amount varies by currency and discount) that references the real product. Accepted cost, deliberately taken: the line-item name on the Checkout page is the default-locale name rather than the parent's, which is the behaviour consumer clubs already had — one mechanism with one known limitation, rather than two mechanisms with two behaviours.
- **A club's Stripe product is allowed to drift, and the backfill script is the fix.** Camps and events reconcile their Stripe product on **every** purchase; a consumer club resolves its price from a cache that returns early, so its product is only revisited on the **first sale** and on a **price change**. A club renamed, retimed or moved to another spoken language can therefore carry stale name, date and language values on Stripe indefinitely, and re-running the backfill is how they are brought back in line. The one field that cannot silently drift is the tax category, because nothing changes it after creation. Lifting the reconcile above the price cache was rejected: it would add a Stripe round trip to every club checkout, on the customer-blocking path, to refresh cosmetic metadata. **A stale product cannot make Stripe misreport a past sale** — a Stripe product is the current catalogue entry, while the invoice and the charge snapshot the tax actually applied at the moment of sale and never change.
- **Keep pricing/checkout code simple first** — prefer Stripe's built-ins (proration, `billing_cycle_anchor`, coupons) over re-implementing its math. Non-negotiable regardless: webhook idempotency (`stripe_event_id` unique on every `payments` row), `FOR UPDATE` seat counting, every incoming money movement recorded, server-recomputed prices. Refunds are the one money movement we do **not** record locally — they were a write-only ledger and the table was dropped; Stripe is the system of record for them. An admin issues one as a **credit note against the invoice**, never as a raw refund, because only a credit note reverses the VAT and the discount — the runbook is in `docs/runbooks/stripe.md`.

### Seat hold vs club access (partly forward-looking)

A participation carries **two independent rights**: the **seat hold** (occupies `seat_count`) and **club access** (voice room, schedule, content). They usually move together; a failed sub payment blocks access immediately but holds the seat for a grace window (`ACCESS_GRACE_DAYS`) before release. Access is a runtime check (`participation_access_state`); seat is long-lived state. *The grace gates and `invoice.payment_failed` wiring are spec'd but not all shipped — verify against code before depending on them.*

### Sessions

- **A session is a `(group, product-local calendar date)` pair — it belongs to the group, not the product.** Two groups on one club run their own sessions, take their own attendance, and write their own reports. Keying on the local *date* rather than a UTC instant or a slot start time is what lets a record survive the commonest admin edit there is (a time-of-day fix); only a weekday move can orphan one.
- **One session per group per local day is a deliberate architectural bet, not an incidental key.** It blocks a group having a morning *and* an afternoon session on the same day — we don't run products that way and don't plan to — and in exchange the key survives every schedule edit but a weekday move, an entry has a stable identity before any row exists, and lazy materialization needs no reconciler. The cost is recorded on the unique constraint itself: revisiting multi-slot days means revisiting this key and every id derived from it.
- **Sessions materialize lazily; schedule math stays the only source of the *plan*.** Dates are still computed from `start_date`/`end_date` + `schedule_slots`; a row is written only when there is something to hold — a report, a note, or an attendance mark. Admins edit dates, times and weekdays freely and nothing needs migrating, because a session with no records is never a row. Extending a term is still one `UPDATE`: no regenerate step, no reconciliation pass.
- **Records beat projections, and an orphaned row is history rather than a mistake.** Reads merge derived occurrences with stored rows on the date key: where both exist the row wins outright, including its stored start and end, and a row the schedule no longer projects still renders. History doesn't retroactively change because the plan was edited.
- **A row snapshots its scheduled start/end at materialization and never re-derives them — and the *server* derives those instants.** A client sends only the date, so it can neither invent a time nor disagree with the schedule about one. Rows also carry created-by/updated-by and timestamps: attendance is the gedu's confirmation that they ran the session, and is what they're paid on, which turns the problem from "nag gedus to record" into "audit what they recorded".
- **Attendance is explicit and tri-state — present, absent, or *unanswered* — and unanswered is the absence of a mark, never a silent absent.** One mark per (session, participant), written **one at a time**, so two gedus marking different people in the same session can't clobber each other; reverting someone to unanswered *deletes* the mark rather than storing a third value, and partial states save by design. **Marks open at the session's scheduled start and the server enforces the boundary** — the standard pattern is a roll call as the session begins, recorded right there while the gedu can see who is in the room. A session that has **not started yet** may hold notes (forward planning), never a mark: nothing has started, so there is nothing to have attended. Note that "not started yet" is narrower than "future" — the feeds' *future* kind flips at the session's end, so the session in progress is a future entry and does take marks, which is the whole roll-call case (see below).
- **Completeness is judged against the *current* roster, because that's the only roster there is.** There is deliberately no participation-history table and no enrollment-at-the-time derivation — "who was enrolled then" is knowledge we don't have and choose not to fake. Accepted consequence, chosen with eyes open: a child joining a long-running group makes previously-complete past sessions read incomplete again.
- **Two markdown fields per session, two audiences, never merged:** the **session report** is family-facing (what parents and gamers read on the family product page, and what will be emailed to them in a later phase); the **gedu note** is gedu + admin only. A note written under an assumption of privacy can never be retro-published. **Links are blocked by policy in both** — a gedu must not send parents or gamers off-site — so the renderer's allow-list carries no anchor and the editor can't produce one; a markdown link renders as its plain text on every surface, including the future email.
- **The enforcement epoch gates what is *owed*, never what is *editable*.** It's a constant in code (`src/lib/constants/session-epoch.ts`, value `2026-08-31`) — not a column, not admin-configurable, not a rolling window — compared as a product-local calendar date. Work owed starts at `max(product start, epoch)`, so a club that has run for two years owes nothing for its history. A session dated before the epoch never shows "needs attention" and never counts into a dashboard badge, however incomplete it is; it stays fully recordable and editable back to the product's start, with the same editors as any other past session. That's why write validation carries no epoch floor — only the attention count does.
- **There is one expansion rule: a session is any date inside the term that lands on a slot weekday, and nothing subtracts from it.** Three implementations of it exist today, in two languages: the shared occurrence expander in `src/lib/` that both session feeds are built from, the admin dashboard's own independent weekday walk in `src/components/admin/dashboard/`, and — a validator rather than a full expansion — the SQL weekday check a session date is put past on write. They agree, which is what keeps a feed from offering a date its own validator would refuse, so **when cancellation/substitution is built, all three must be unified in one pass** rather than each learning the new subtraction separately.
- **Write validation is deliberately loose:** at or after the product start, within the horizon the feed will ever show, and on a weekday the current schedule uses. Stricter buys little — an admin edit can orphan any row a day later anyway — and risks refusing a legitimate write-up in the minutes after a schedule fix.
- **Two schema concepts were reserved with zero behaviour and have since been dropped:** a didn't-run flag and a needs-substitute flag. They were held on the session row against the cancellation/substitution flows, on the reasoning that reserving two booleans costs nothing while adding columns to a populated table later costs a migration each. That trade was re-taken once the family surfaces landed: the feature is cut from the gedu UI and is not being built, and a column nothing reads still advertises a behaviour to everyone who meets the schema. The backend stopped advertising it. Cancellation brings its own columns when it is designed.
- **Session writes are open to an admin, or to a gedu assigned to the group** — the report, the staff note, the register, the group's standing notes and the site's. Each writer's guard admits either role, and an admin skips the assignment half of the gate and *nothing else*: the roster check that refuses a mark aimed at somebody who holds no seat in the group, the roll-call boundary, the writable-date rule and both refusals on the report send bind an admin identically, because those are rules about the integrity of the record rather than about who is looking at it. The audit trail is not relaxed either — the last-editor stamp is whoever wrote, so an admin correcting a tick is named on the card. Admins reach all of it from the product's own page, over the gedu components themselves; the workaround it replaced was creating a second gedu account, assigning it to the group and leaving it there for ever, which is a fiction in the data that nobody ever cleans up. **The two feed reads stay role-split**: a gedu's answers "my group's workspace", an admin's is keyed by *product* and hands back every group on it at once, which is the question a page with a group selector actually asks. Peer-group feeds are still not readable by a gedu in v1, and the schema doesn't block opening them: that's a change to one predicate, not a redesign.
- **Concurrent edits are last-write-wins, accepted.** Two gedus editing the same session, group, or site notes at once means the later save overwrites — no locking, no merge — and each write path says so at the point it overwrites. Attendance is the one place the failure is *structurally* avoided rather than accepted, by the per-mark shape above.
- Times are product-local: `products.timezone` (IANA), `schedule_slots.start_time` is wall-clock (stable across DST), and a session's date is the calendar date in that zone. The zone is the admin's to pick on the product form — from the zones the seeded countries declare, defaulting to Helsinki — and it stays editable after creation: because the schedule is stored as a wall clock, changing the zone re-resolves every session the schedule still *projects* to that same clock face in the new one, which is the correction an admin who picked the wrong zone needs. **A session that has already been materialized does not move**, and that is the half worth stating out loud: a row is written only once a report, a note or an attendance mark needs somewhere to live, and it snapshots its start and end at that moment and never re-derives them — so a session already held keeps the times it was held at while the rest of the term follows the new zone. The edit form's hint says exactly that, because an admin fixing a zone would otherwise expect their history to be rewritten too. Render in the viewer's zone with a "product zone / your time" label when they differ.
- **Reserved RPC names: `record_attendance` is now taken** by the per-mark write above. `cancel_session`, `reschedule_session` and `request_substitute` / `assign_substitute` remain reserved for the cancellation/substitution feature.
- **A session in progress is the *current* session on both feeds, and owes nothing yet.** Three questions, three answers, and the staff and family surfaces agree on every one — they are one timeline read by two audiences and must never disagree about which side of the present a session is on. *Which kind of entry it is* turns on the **end** instant: a session stays "future" until its last minute has passed, so the one running right now sits at the head of the feed as the current session, live-tagged, rather than filed under history. Both feeds compute the live tag as the same conjunction (future kind, started, not yet ended, exclusive end boundary), so the kind flips on the exact tick the tag stops being live and no dead zone opens between them. *Editability* turns on the **start** instant: the register opens when the session begins and the server accepts marks from that same moment, so the live entry carries the record editor exactly as a finished one does and the roll-call flow works while the session runs. *Being owed* turns on the **end** instant as well — the client's per-entry alert and the SQL attention count both wait for the session to finish, because an hour the gedu is still standing in is not yet work outstanding.
  - **History, because the old rule was reasonable and its replacement is the point.** The staff feed used to split its kind on the **start**, which made a running session sort into the past. That kind was doing double duty as "may I take the register yet" — making the running session past was simply how it reached the record editor, and roll call during the club is the whole purpose of that editor. Two questions wearing one flag. A daily 8:00–23:00 camp made the cost plain: fifteen hours of the workspace calling the session the gedu was teaching "last time" and naming tomorrow as next, while a parent reading the same hour was correctly told it was today's. Editability is asked of the clock directly now, which freed the kind rule to mean one thing on both feeds. The two builders stay separate for the **privacy** boundary — they emit different shapes, one carrying staff-only notes, the whole roster's marks and an owed flag — not for kind semantics, and the classifiers agreeing is no invitation to fuse them.
- **The family-facing half is built, and the Padlet is retired end to end.** A parent or gamer opens one page per (participant × product) from their My SOG card and reads the group's stored reports there, so the external wall the old family surfaces linked out to is gone — the render sites, the admin form field and the `products.padlet_url` column with them. (`schema.sql` may still show that column until its CI regeneration runs on the next dev merge — a stale snapshot, not a contradiction of the migration.) The gedu-only lesson-material link is a *different* field with no backfill from the Padlet — the Padlet held family session notes, the material link is lesson content. **It is not a column on `products` and must not become one:** `products` carries an anon SELECT grant and PostgREST lets a caller name the columns it wants, so anything staff-only sitting there is world-readable whatever the comment above it says. It lives in `product_staff_details` — admin-only RLS, no anon grant at all — which gedus reach only through the feed RPC, and which is where the next staff-only product field belongs too.
- **The site address is not a gedu's to write.** The site-notes RPC takes the two notes and nothing else; the address belongs to the location record and is an admin's to edit. It used to be a parameter, with the workspace echoing its cached copy back on every save — so a gedu saving a note against a page loaded before an admin's correction silently reverted it, and any assigned gedu could rewrite the address of a building they teach at outright. The RPC now preserves whatever address is stored and returns it for display only.
- **The site notes are not part of the product form, and an admin edits them where a gedu does — on the product's page, beside the group's standing notes.** They are site-scoped, so every product at that building reads and writes the same two paragraphs, and they save out of band: a field that committed the instant its own little Save button was pressed sat inside a form that committed nothing until the bottom of the page, which surprised everyone who met it. Editing them through the same panel the gedu uses also means one write path rather than two. **That panel is the whole site — name, address and both notes — and it is the only component rendering one anywhere staff meet a site**, the admin site page included, so there is one arrangement of those four fields rather than one per surface. **Which of them a viewer may write is decided by which save callbacks the surface supplies, never by a role flag inside the panel:** everyone gets the notes save, an admin surface also gets the details save, and what is not supplied renders read-only. Behind that one Save the writes stay separate, because their owners are — the two notes go through the shared RPC that takes no name and no address, the name and the address go through two admin routes that take nothing else, only fields that actually changed are sent, and each route leaves an absent field alone rather than writing it null. So neither writer can blank what the other just saved, no caller has to echo back a cached copy of a field it is not editing, and a half-failed save leaves exactly the failed field dirty.

### Groups & capacity

- **Groups are an admin-only cohort layer; parents never see them.** Every type uses them. A product has 0+ named groups, each with 0+ gedus and 0+ participations.
- **Capacity lives on the product, not groups.** Admins balance participants across groups by hand.
- **A new participation lands `group_id = NULL` — the unassigned inbox admins work through — unless the placement is forced, in which case the enrollment writer makes it.** It is forced on exactly one shape: a product that **charges nothing** (free, or a municipality club invoiced off-platform) **and has exactly one group**. There is no decision left in that combination — every chip arriving in the inbox would be dragged into the same column, and on a municipality club that is a whole school year's intake of clerical work — so the seat is written straight into that group, and the trigger that stamps `group_joined_at` from `group_id` treats it exactly like a hand placement. **Zero groups has nowhere to put anybody and two or more is a real decision**, so both still land in the inbox, as does every **paid** seat: a paid row is written later by the Stripe webhook, on a different transaction from the one that took the request, and is deliberately left alone. Whether the single group has a gedu assigned is not consulted — an unstaffed group is still the only place the seat can go. The count is taken under the product row lock the writers already hold, which is the same lock the group editor takes, so it cannot read a half-applied group edit. Rows already sitting in the inbox when the rule arrived were left where they were. Deleting a group resets its participations to unassigned.
- **The admin groups panel communicates the rule by *not drawing* the unassigned section, never by captioning it.** An empty box that nothing can ever land in reads as a fault; a box that is not there says the same thing without a sentence to translate. So the section is hidden on exactly one combination — the product qualifies for forced placement (charges nothing AND has exactly one group) **and** the inbox is empty — and is drawn in every other case, unchanged: paid, no groups, several groups. **Anyone actually unassigned wins over the hide, always**, because a gamer with no group who is off screen is a gamer nobody can seat; a non-empty inbox draws the section even on a qualifying product, which is how rows predating the rule stay reachable. Hidden-and-empty is a stable state rather than a race: every event that could put a row in a qualifying product's inbox either disqualifies the product (deleting the only group leaves zero groups, which disqualifies on its own — and where that group had members, they are reset to unassigned and fill the inbox as well; adding a second group disqualifies on the spot) or fills the inbox, and each draws the section back. **The two inputs that can *hide* the section — the group count and the inbox count — come from the one snapshot that draws the panel**, so they cannot disagree and the first paint is already right. The billing half comes from the product row, a separate read that can be stale relative to that snapshot, and it can never hide anything on its own because the inbox count is checked first: a stale "paid" only draws a section that did not need drawing, and a stale "free" cannot hide rows the database really left unassigned, because those rows short-circuit the answer to "show" before billing is consulted. A row's own presence outranks the billing half in every staleness direction. What flips the answer is an admin edit — adding or deleting a group, moving the last inbox row out, or changing the billing mode — though not necessarily *this* admin's: another's edit arrives on a refetch, the same way the panel's chips already reorder on refocus.
- **Hiding the section also removes its drop target on a qualifying product, and that is accepted rather than worked around.** A member dragged off a column there has the removal zone (and the waitlist, where the product opens one) and nowhere else. To park a gamer outside a group on such a product, an admin adds a second group — the product stops qualifying and the section appears — drags the gamer out of their column and into it, then deletes the now-empty extra group; the product qualifies again but the section stays, because the inbox is no longer empty. That is the answer to a rare need, deliberately in place of a drag-only parking target that would exist solely to be dropped on.
- **A gedu is on at most one group per product** (unique constraint); they can be on many *different* products.
- Cohorts differing only in gedu / voice room = multiple groups in one product; cohorts differing in schedule = separate products.

### Waitlist

- **Order is derived from `participations.waitlisted_at` (`ORDER BY waitlisted_at, id`), never a stored rank** — removing or promoting a row needs no renumbering. (`join_waitlist` stamps `clock_timestamp()` under the lock so concurrent joins get distinct ranks.)
- **A waitlist is available behind a cap on any product type.** The queue itself is billing-agnostic — joining one takes no money and creates nothing but a row — so nothing about it needs to be gated by type.
- **No automatic promotion.** An admin promotes/demotes manually from the groups panel: `promote_from_waitlist` (→ active, place in a group/unassigned, **no seat-count gate** — a deliberate capacity override) and `demote_to_waitlist` (→ back of the line, refused when the participation carries a live subscription — see below). Both admin-gated, under the product lock. Promotion seats a family whether or not they can still come, which is what the seat offer below exists to ask first.

#### The seat offer

A seat opens mid-term and the queue behind it is months old. **Promoting the next family in line hands them a seat they may no longer want**, and nothing then tells anybody the seat is dead. The seat offer is the question that goes first: an admin invites one waitlisted family, and the seat moves only if they say yes.

- **Two stored columns on the participation, and everything else derived.** One records when the offer was sent; the other records that staff have already been told it lapsed. Whether an offer is *live* is never stored — it is `sent + five days > now`, evaluated wherever anybody asks, which is why nothing has to come along later and turn a flag off. The window is a single number that exists twice on purpose, in the database and in TypeScript, each side carrying a comment naming its twin; changing it is a two-file edit and a database test asserts the two agree. Every reader — the admin's waitlist card, the family's own card, the emailed link, the acceptance itself — derives from that one stamp, so no two of them can disagree about whether an offer still stands.
- **It is offered only where a yes can be honoured on the spot: a no-charge product with exactly one group.** No-charge, because accepting a paid seat would need a checkout in the middle of the answer — a different feature. Exactly one group, because accepting has to place the child somewhere and a family is never asked to choose between groups. The refusal lives in the database; the panel asks the same two questions only to decide what to draw, and the two halves of the answer are treated differently: a wrong group count is fixable on the very page the admin is looking at and is said out loud, while a paid product is a property of the product that no arrangement of groups changes, so nothing is drawn and nothing is said.
- **The offer is a question, not a grant.** Sending one stamps the row and mails the family; the row stays `waitlisted` and their place in line is untouched. A second send while an offer is live is refused inside the same lock and **deliberately reports the original send instant**, so a second press cannot move a deadline the family is already reading in their inbox — and no second mail goes out. The admin panel does not offer the control at all while an offer stands, which is the same guard in a form a reader can see.
- **Accepting always honours the seat.** There is no seat-count gate — the same capacity override promotion makes, with a stronger claim behind it, because the family was asked. If the product stopped having exactly one group while they were deciding, the seat is still granted and lands unassigned: a placement question is ours, and not a reason to withdraw an invitation. **A product flipped from no-charge to paid mid-offer honours the free seat too, and that grandfathering is deliberate**: the answer re-resolves where the seat lands but never re-asks whether it should have been offered, because the offer went out saying the seat cost nothing and an accepted invite is always honoured — the same stance a billing-mode flip takes everywhere else, where existing participants keep the terms they enrolled on. **The grandfathering covers the offer's terms and stops at the product itself: the answer re-checks the product by id, and a product that has been deleted or cancelled by the time the family answers ends the answer as the same indistinguishable "no longer open" everything else does** — the one fact an honoured invite always requires is that the product it names still exists and still stands, because an invitation to a cancelled club is an invitation to nothing and seating a family into it would be worse for them than refusing. A product that has merely run out of dates is not covered by that: it still exists and nothing has been withdrawn, and whether an offer should be sendable or answerable on one is an open question rather than a settled rule. **Declining hard-deletes the row** (matching the parent-facing leave) and mails staff at once, because the identifiers the mail names cannot be read after the delete. **Staff here means every admin account, resolved from the role column at send time rather than a hardcoded inbox** — the same recipient list the feedback notification uses, and for the same reason: what the mail asks for is done in the admin UI, so it goes to the people who can do it. It is one builder with two reasons, and everything the two share is written to be true of both — the reason lives in the subject, the heading and one sentence, and the rest of the mail says only that the offer is over and the seat wants the next family.
- **An offer ends by having its stamp move, and that is the whole revocation story.** Answering and re-offering change or remove the value the emailed link was signed over, so a used link, a stale tab and a superseded offer all fail the same compare-and-swap — with no revocation table and no second thing to keep in step. Expiry is the exception and leaves the stamp alone, because the link has to go on working for the one answer the window does not govern. An offer whose window closed with no answer is re-offerable, and re-offering clears the old expiry notification with it.
- **The window binds accepting, and nothing else.** Its whole job is to stop a seat being *claimed* after we have given up waiting and offered it to somebody else; none of that reasoning reaches a family telling us they cannot come, which is the one piece of news we most want and the one that frees a row. So a decline succeeds for as long as the participation exists, late or not — on the emailed link, whose expired page carries the decline and not the accept, and on the family's own lapsed card in My SOG, where the same button stays live. **A late decline mails nobody.** Staff were already told this offer went unanswered when it was swept, and a second mail would raise a case an admin has finished with; the answer that arrives inside the window keeps its mail exactly as before. The two are told apart inside the answering RPC, which is the only place the stored stamp and the clock are read together, and the family reads the same thank-you either way.
- **Silence costs the place in line — spent at the moment it is noticed, not at the five-day mark.** An offer that ran out unanswered is a turn that came up and was not taken, and a family who kept their position through it would be asked first again on the next seat while everybody behind them waited another full window for an answer that never came. So the statement that claims the expiry notification also re-stamps the row's waitlist ordering key, moving that family to the back of the queue. Because expiry is observed rather than scheduled (below), that re-stamp lands when somebody first looks — a day or two of grace, and the honest description of the mechanism rather than an approximation of it. Both offer stamps survive the claim: one is what the emailed link still compares against, so a late decline keeps working and the landing page can still tell a lapsed offer apart from a spent one.
- **Silence is noticed lazily, and exactly once — there is no cron job.** Nothing in a database watches a clock, so the moment of noticing is somebody arriving at a surface that would care: an admin opening the dashboard or a product's groups panel, or a family answering an offer — from the mail or from My SOG, and a lapsed link counts as an answer for this purpose. Each of those claims lapsed-and-unreported offers and marks them in one statement, and mails staff about whatever it claimed. **The observation points are funnelled, deliberately, so they cannot scatter**: the claim RPC is service-role-only, every server-side trigger goes through the one claim-and-notify helper beside the seat-offer mail senders, and every admin surface goes through the one sweep-on-mount hook and its admin route — so the complete list of surfaces that can trigger the sweep is always the callers of those two names, recoverable by grep. A new surface that should notice lapsed offers is wired by calling one of them, never by touching the RPC itself. Two admins landing in the same second therefore produce one mail between them, and usually an empty set. What this costs is latency — if nobody looks for a week, staff hear a week late. What it buys is that the notification cannot drift out of step with the thing it is about, because there is one predicate and it is evaluated when somebody asks.
- **How much a trigger may claim follows the credential it arrived on, and only an admin's sweeps the platform.** The claim takes an optional participation id: omitted it claims every lapsed unreported offer, and that mode belongs to the admin surfaces, which are entitled to observe the whole platform. A family's trigger passes the one row their credential names and claims nothing else — the emailed link is a signed token naming one participation whose *signature* never expires (the five-day window is checked against the row, not against the token's age), so an old link out of a forwarded inbox is a trigger that goes on working forever, and unscoped it was a permanent unthrottled way to make the platform write and to fan staff mail out about families the clicker has nothing to do with. The in-app answer is scoped on the same rule rather than because its session is doubted: a credential that names one row carries one row's worth of authority. What the scoping costs is exactly the latency the lazy design already accepts — everybody else's lapsed offers wait for an admin to look.
- **The claim commits before the mail, so a failed send loses that notice for good — and that is accepted.** Making the claim and the mark one statement is what buys exactly-once; the price is that there is nothing left to retry against, because no later sweep will claim the row again. The mail is a nudge and the attention queue is the record: an open seat with no live offer against it is precisely what the dashboard flags, so the product raises itself again without the mail. Holding the claim open across a third-party call would buy the retry back at the cost of duplicate mail under concurrency and a lock held across the network. The sends are also batched a few at a time rather than fired at once, because a sweep of a platform nobody has looked at for a fortnight is an unbounded fan-out otherwise.
- **The dashboard's waitlist line subtracts live offers from a product's open seats.** A product drops out of the attention queue once every open seat carries an offer — the families have been asked and there is nothing to do but wait — and a decline or an expiry puts it back on its own. The line states the offer count alongside the queue and the seats, so an admin can see why two open seats need only one more invitation.
- **The family answers from their inbox or from My SOG, through two routes with two different credentials.** The emailed path is authorized by a signed token and has no session; the in-app path is authorized by the session and has no token, and proves the participation belongs to the caller under their own policies before it acts. They are deliberately not one handler that accepts either, because that is one handler where a missing token can be read as "must be the session path". Both check the five-day window at answer time rather than trusting anything the caller sent. The parent's card puts a confirmation in front of declining and none in front of accepting: accepting is recoverable, declining spends a place in a queue that took months to reach.
- **What a dead link is told depends on whether we can read it, and the signature is the whole of that line.** A token whose HMAC does not verify gets one generic page saying the link did not work, which is what stops the public endpoint confirming that any given participation id exists. A token whose HMAC *does* verify is one we minted for one exact offer, so its holder may be told that offer is over: every consumed shape — they accepted, an admin promoted them, they declined, they left, a newer invitation replaced this one — arrives as a single "this link has already been used", pointing at My SOG. It deliberately never says *which*, both because the row is often gone and cannot say, and because a page carrying no session should not narrate a family's history back at them. What this fixes is the case that prompted it: a parent re-opening the mail of an offer they had already accepted used to read that it had expired.
- **Only one product shape is billed as a recurring subscription, and that decides which refusals bind — not which types may carry a waitlist.** Paid consumer clubs are monthly subscriptions; paid camps and paid events are single upfront payments; municipality clubs are invoiced off-platform; a free product of any type takes no money at all. So a subscription row exists only for a paid consumer-club participation — the distinction that matters here is *"this participation requires a subscription"*, **not** *"this product is paid"*, which is why a paid camp with a waitlist is perfectly safe and why a free club is not a special case.
- **A waitlisted row must never carry a live subscription, and `demote_to_waitlist` refuses any participation that has one.** Joining a waitlist never creates a subscription (nobody has paid for anything yet), so the only way to reach that state is to take an already-active member — who does have one — and move them onto the waitlist. **"Live" is a state, not the existence of a subscription record.** A subscription that has died — cancelled at Stripe, or exhausted by dunning — updates its record in place rather than dropping it, and the deletion webhook that would tidy up never fires for that path, so a refusal keyed to mere existence would hold the seat hostage for the rest of the product's life over a subscription nobody is being billed on. The predicate excludes the dead ones, and the demote refusal, the admin-removal refusal and the admin panel's own snapshot all read the same one so the three can never disagree about whether a seat is releasable. That refusal is what makes the parent-facing leave safe to implement as an outright delete: the subscription link is `ON DELETE CASCADE`, so deleting such a row would drop the only record of a subscription that keeps billing at Stripe. **The refusal is keyed to the participation's own subscription rather than to the product's type or billing mode, and it lives in the database rather than in the drag handler, because the row it protects is parent-deletable** — the dangerous caller here is the parent, not the admin, and a rule that binds only callers who came through the drag handler is not a rule the database holds. The panel does put an explanatory dialog in front of the drag as well, but that is an affordance; the RPC's refusal is the enforcement, and it keeps working after any billing-mode flip precisely because it never reads billing mode.
- **Turning the waitlist off deletes the queue behind it, silently.** The flag goes off two ways — unticking it, or uncapping the product, from which the form derives it — and both save paths delete every `waitlisted` row on the product. Keeping them would strand the families (the admin panel draws its waitlist column only while the flag is on, so nothing could promote or remove them) and promoting them instead would hand out seats with no subscription behind them; deletion is clean because the same edit *opens* seats, so anyone dropped can come back through the front door — instantly on a free product, through checkout on a paid one, which is the step that creates the subscription a promotion never could. **Nobody is told**: no confirmation, no warning, no email, by deliberate decision — the edit is expected to be an accident rather than an eviction, and what is lost is a place in a line for a product that is no longer full. **One exception: a waitlisted row carrying a live subscription is never deleted**, because the subscription link cascades and dropping it would orphan billing that keeps running — the same hazard the demote and admin-removal refusals exist for, read through the same liveness predicate. That state is a webhook race and effectively unreachable, so it is skipped in silence. The delete is keyed to the flag's saved value rather than to it changing, so it also clears any queue stranded before the rule existed.
- **`get_waitlist_position`** is the parent/gamer "you're #N" read: `SECURITY DEFINER` to count past the caller's RLS, but **owner-authorized** and returns only the integer. Its **set-valued sibling, `get_my_waitlist_positions`**, answers for every row the caller is party to in one snapshot — what the dashboards use, because a per-card call would be an N+1 whose answers are also read at N different instants.
- **The dashboard read is split by status, and the two halves must stay disjoint.** The sessions read filters to `active` — including the unplaced rows, which have no group and therefore no schedule to expand, because a bought seat awaiting placement is still a thing the family is owed a card for; the waitlist read filters to `waitlisted`. A row appearing in both, or in neither, is the bug to watch for when either filter changes.
- **A parent may leave a waitlist; a gamer may not.** `leave_my_waitlist_spot` is authorized to the purchasing parent (`customer_id = auth.uid()`) — the affordance is a quiet link on the parent's copy of the card and is absent from the child's, and the database enforces the same split rather than trusting it. Being keyed to the customer, it already covers a parent leaving a queue they joined **for themselves** on a for-parents product, with no case of its own. It answers "not yours" and "not real" identically so the id space can't be probed, and **deletes** the row (matching `cancel_participation`) because `participation_status` has no terminal member to move it to.

### Lifecycle & listing

**Status and listing are two axes and answer two different questions.** Status is the product's lifecycle — where it is in its own life. Listing is where the product is advertised. Neither is a permission, and **nothing about buying a place is decided by listing**: a purchase is gated by the effective status, the seat cap and the registration window, and by nothing else.

- **Effective status is derived, not cron-driven.** `status` stores admin facts only (`pending`, `cancelled`, `completed`, and the `running` override); `pending → running` (start_date reached AND any threshold met) and `running → completed` (end_date passed) are computed at read time, along with `expired` — a pending product whose end date passed without its start conditions ever being met. **The TS helper (`effective-status.ts`) and the SQL `effective_status()` twin must stay in lockstep** — RLS/list filters call the SQL form.
- **Status is also what closes a product to the public.** The product read predicate lets anyone read a product in a published status (`pending` or `running`); a cancelled or completed one is readable only by its admins, its enrolled families and its assigned gedus. That is the only lever that hides a product from people who have nothing to do with it.
- **`is_visible` means "listed", and only that.** An unlisted product appears on no public browse page — not the shop, not the schools pages — but a parent holding its URL can open the page, read it, and buy a place exactly as they could a listed one. That is the point: an ad campaign's landing product, a cohort offered to one school, an early-access club handed round by link. **The filter lives in the browse queries, never in the database's read rule**, and putting it back into a single-product read would break the direct link the state exists for.
- **Three consequences of that were weighed and accepted (owner decision, Aug 2026); they are trade-offs, not gaps to be closed later.**
  1. An unlisted product is **enumerable through the anon Data API** — a read rule is a predicate, not a search filter, so anything readable can be listed by querying the table directly. This is obscurity, not secrecy.
  2. A shared or leaked link **can be picked up by a search engine**. The mitigation is broader than the visibility feature (owner decision, Aug 2026): **search engines and AI crawlers may discover only the shop browse page.** Every product detail page — listed or not, at every public URL that renders one, since one product row is reachable at more than one — and the entire schools tree emit an unconditional `noindex, nofollow`. That makes the rule static (no per-product read) and moots any canonical question between a product's two URLs. It is deliberately a tag rather than a robots.txt entry — a disallowed URL is never fetched, so the crawler would never read the tag and the bare URL could still be indexed off an external link.
  3. **There is no truly-invisible state any more.** A product still being written is merely unlisted, so a link-holder can see and buy something unfinished. Nothing in the model prevents this; the answer is to leave a half-built product's registration closed or its status un-published, not to expect listing to hide it.
- **`draft` was removed in Aug 2026, as dead code.** Nothing ever wrote it — the admin form has always created products as `pending`, and the "save an incomplete product" flow the value was reserved for was never built; both hosted databases held zero rows in it on the day it was dropped. Its one enforced meaning was "a draft is never visible", which stopped meaning anything the moment unlisted became readable. **If that save-incomplete flow is ever genuinely built, the state comes back then**, with the constraint escape hatches and the write path that make it real — a status nothing produces is a question every reader of the type has to answer for no one.
- The only manual lifecycle lever is `start_product` (the under-threshold override) — spec'd, not yet built. Cancellation has no built flow either; any refunds it implies are manual, in Stripe (see §Seat gate — no state may promise one).

### Topic, location, voice

- **`topic` is a fixed Postgres enum, and one flat axis** — there is deliberately no game/subject split in the model; adding a value is a migration, not a row, and there is no admin topic CRUD. The enum stores *only* the value — display order, labels and each topic's optional `info` block (an age rating where one exists, where to get the software) all live in code (`src/lib/products/topics.ts`), not the DB. Labels are brand literals (never translated — every topic is a proper noun), and the presence of a topic's `info` block — not any category — is what makes the product detail page render its "About {name}" card. The parent-facing prose *about* a topic is translated even though its name isn't, so a new topic still needs message entries in every locale. In the UI the axis is labelled "Subject" (per-locale wording, e.g. fr "Univers") while code says `topic` — the same deliberate divergence as "My SOG" vs "dashboard". This list is deliberately not enumerated here — it went stale twice; read the module.
- **Location rules:** in-person → `location_id` is a `site` (required); online muni → a country/region/municipality (required); online non-muni → NULL. **Muni residency is honour-system intent, not enforced** — never use `municipality_club` as a proxy for "free to all."
- **Voice rooms exist iff `is_remote`, one per group.** A participant sees only their own group's room, and the membership check is the seat itself, so a parent holding one reaches their room the same way a gamer reaches theirs — **from their own card, with no account switch and no moderator capabilities**, while a child's room still requires switching to that child. A gedu sees every group's room on a product they're assigned to (emergency coverage); an unassigned participation has none.

## RLS topology

- **Admin = full access** on every table. **Writes are RPC-gated** — tables mutated by `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs grant no INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE to `authenticated`.
- **What a family may never see is a fixed list, and it is narrower than "everything staff-side".** Never a session's gedu note, never the roster or another participant's name or mark, never anyone else's email, never `site_staff_details`, never a product's gedu-only lesson-material link, never a completeness or owed state. What they *do* see of the cohort layer is deliberate and small: their own participant's group by name (their child's, or their own seat's), that group's note for families, that group's gedus as first names, and that participant's attendance mark. All of it arrives through a self-scoping `SECURITY DEFINER` read rather than a table grant, so the RLS posture on `product_groups` is unchanged by any of it — the visibility decision lives in what that function returns. (The public product detail page is a different surface answering a different question, and lists the product's gedus as `DISTINCT gedu_id` across all its groups.)
- **Gamers have no payment visibility**; customers see their own `payments`/`family_subscriptions` via `customer_id = auth.uid()`.
- **Gedus read products regardless of listing or status** (they may be assigned to an unlisted or a cancelled one).
- `product_subscription_prices` (Stripe Price IDs) is not a public catalog — parents only see the computed display price from `product_prices`.
- Enforced by the catalog checks in `tests/db/access-control.test.ts` (RLS on every table; only allowlisted functions callable by anon/authenticated).

## Parent surfaces

Two parallel entry points, **never cross-linked** — one canonical URL per product, produced by a single `productDetailPath`-style helper:

- **`/shop`** — consumer browse (`/shop/[id]` detail). Covers consumer clubs, camps and events behind a required Clubs|Camps|Events type filter. **Muni clubs are excluded** — they need a location to be discovered by, which this surface doesn't collect.
- **`/schools`** — municipality discovery, location-first (`/schools` → `/schools/[municipalityName]` → `/schools/[municipalityName]/[id]`). Lists only `municipality_club` rows that are listed + pending/running.

**Rule: a discovery surface that doesn't collect a location can't surface muni clubs.** Every product has `registration_opens_at` (the "ticket drop"); the detail page is pre-open (countdown) / open / closed-or-waitlist.

`is_visible` gates whether parents can **find** a product on these surfaces — never whether they can open or buy it, which a direct link does for a listed and an unlisted product alike. Already-enrolled families keep their product on their My SOG dashboard regardless of either axis.

**Rule: a product's picture is one catalogue entry — one stored image any number of products may share — cropped to the project's one 3:2 ratio on every surface that shows it — family-facing and admin alike — filling the frame rather than letterboxed inside it, and never re-ratioed per surface.** The browse card's media block, the detail hero, the purchase confirmation's summary row, the admin product list rows and the admin product page all paint the same crop through the one shared banner component; the caller chooses a width, never a ratio. A surface free to pick its own ratio is one where the same photograph gets cropped two different ways — for a parent, a picture recognised on a card then unrecognisable on the page it opened; for an admin, worse: the admin surfaces are how admins see and think about products, so an admin thumb cropped differently has them approving a picture families never meet (this closed the brief admin-square exception, owner ruling Aug 2026). A product with **no** picture gets the SOG wordmark at that same ratio, so a grid never develops short cards and no page reflows around whether a photo exists yet. The admin upload control previews that same crop rather than the whole file, so what an admin approves is what a family sees.

**Enrolled families have their own pair of surfaces, and they are not discovery.** My SOG (`/parent`, `/gamer`) lists one card per enrollment — the parent's grouped by child and then, when they hold seats of their own, a further section titled with their own first name; the child's grouped by type noun — and each *placed* card opens a family product page at `/parent/{clubs,camps,events}/[id]` or its gamer twin. **Those pages are keyed by participation id, not product id:** the page is per (participant × product), so two siblings in one club get two of them, a parent's own seat gets one more, and every feature queued behind it (planned absences, per-participant notes, a line to the gedu) is per-person too. Only a placed enrollment has a page — a waitlisted or unplaced card has no group behind it and so links nowhere at all, rather than linking somewhere empty.

## Admin surfaces

- Routes per type: `/admin/{consumer-clubs,municipality-clubs,camps,events}` + `/new`, `/[id]` (details), `/[id]/edit`.
- One shared form shell (`product-form.tsx`) → per-section components; pure build/validate in `product-build.ts`; per-type field/shape config in `product-type-config.ts`; pre-prod feature locks in `form-locks.ts`, resolved through one function rather than read as constants — **registration timing is the one thing left that a type unlocks, and municipality clubs are the only type that unlocks it.** Seat caps and waitlists are offered on every type now (see the seat-gate invariant above). Every consumer-facing type is pinned to a registration window that opens right away, and a stored future drop on a pinned type is normalised back to "now" by the next save rather than shown in a chooser the admin cannot move. Nothing in the form reads the lock constants directly; sections and the initial-state builder all go through the one resolver, which is what keeps a single place deciding. `create_product` / `update_product` are atomic (parent row + all child sets in one transaction).
- **Capacity and billing defaults are keyed to the money, so "forgot to cap" cannot happen.** A municipality club must carry a cap — there is no "no seat limit" option for it and a blank number fails validation, which means an existing uncapped muni row will be asked for one on its next edit. A **free** product of any type defaults to limited seats with an empty, required number, so the admin either types one or actively chooses no limit; a **paid** product defaults uncapped, because soft caps are opt-in. Consumer clubs and camps choose free or paid the way events do, defaulting to paid. Flipping the form to free turns capped mode on only if capacity is currently uncapped, and never blanks a number the admin already typed; flipping to paid leaves capacity alone — caps and waitlists are legal on both sides, so nothing needs clearing.
- **A billing-mode flip is unguarded in both directions, deliberately, and the grandfathering it causes is accepted.** An admin may flip a product that already has participants: free enrollees on a product that turns paid keep their seats unbilled, and subscribers on one that turns free keep being billed until they cancel. Nothing breaks or orphans, because the entire subscription lifecycle — portal cancel, the deletion webhook, renewals, dunning, the seat release — is keyed to the Stripe subscription and the participation, and no path on it reads the product's billing mode. A paying family self-migrates by cancelling and re-enrolling free, subject to whatever cap the product now carries. The upfront types have no such path: a family that already paid a camp or event total has nothing to cancel, so the flip leaves their paid seat exactly as it stands and any correction is a manual **credit note against the invoice**, issued by an admin in the Stripe dashboard — never a raw refund, because only a credit note reverses the VAT and the discount (see §Billing).
- **The Audience section carries the two flags, and the age fields hang off one of them.** The form refuses to release the last remaining tick, so the CHECK behind it is a backstop rather than an error an admin can meet. Unticking For gamers **hides the age fields without emptying them** — a mis-click costs nothing while the form is open — and the payload builder derives the pair from the flag on the way out, so the write is the single gate rather than every path to it, and a stored row whose ages and audience disagree is corrected by the next save of anything at all. This is the same shape the waitlist tick uses. Both flags also ride in form state on every save, because `update_product` assigns every editable column on every call (see §Known gaps).
- **Groups + waitlist management lives on the per-product details page, not the form** — one drag-and-drop panel (unassigned inbox + group columns + waitlist), writing through `apply_group_changes` (group structure) and the waitlist RPCs. The waitlist column shows for any waitlist-enabled product, of any type, and the three payment-messy drags — promoting a never-paid waitlister, demoting a subscriber, removing one — are fronted by the dialogs described above rather than by new machinery. The waitlist column also carries the seat offer: an Invite control per queued row on the products that qualify, replaced while an offer stands by the time it has left, and brought back as *Invite again* once a window closes unanswered. Display order is server-side by recency; the client appends optimistically to match, so moves don't flicker.
- **The dashboard's attention queue is product-first, state-prioritized, and deliberately time-blind.** It lists live products carrying at least one thing wrong, and a product with nothing wrong is not on it at all. The ordering is a fixed list of *kinds* of wrong, worst first — a child enrolled with nobody looking after them at the top, a number missing from a form at the bottom — and it sorts both the lines inside one product's card and the products against each other. Product-first because an admin thinks "which product needs me and what is wrong with it", not "show me every unassigned gamer"; a category-first queue would scatter one product's three problems across three lists and make the admin reassemble them.
- **What the ranking measures is who is affected, not what is unset — which is why the same fact can appear twice at different heights.** A group with no educator is near the top when somebody is in it and near the bottom when nobody is: the empty one is a loose end an admin has to close before the term starts, not a child nobody is looking after. It used to be invisible altogether, carved out of the group check on the reasoning that an admin building next term's groups ahead of time has not made a mistake — which is true, and now sets the line's rank rather than hiding the group. A product whose only problem is an empty unstaffed group is in the queue.
- **Time-blindness is a chosen boundary of that queue, not a gap.** Nothing in the ranking reads a start date, so a group with no educator starting tomorrow ranks identically to one starting next month, and a missing fee on a club opening this week still sits below an unassigned child on one opening in the spring. Weighting by imminence is a different design with its own unanswered questions — what horizon counts as urgent, whether a near date may outrank a worse kind of wrong, what a product with no start date does — and it is a future conversation. Recorded here so it is recognised as a decision rather than rediscovered as a bug.

## RPCs

Names by purpose — **read the signatures and bodies in `schema.sql`**. All `SECURITY DEFINER`, gate-locked where they touch seats or money.

- **Participation lifecycle:** `create_participation`, `confirm_paid_participation`, `join_waitlist`, `promote_from_waitlist`, `demote_to_waitlist`, `get_waitlist_position`, `get_my_waitlist_positions`, `leave_my_waitlist_spot`, `cancel_participation`, the seat-offer trio `send_seat_offer` / `respond_seat_offer` / `claim_expired_seat_offer_notifications` (service-role only — the family answers from a public page with no session to guard on, so all three share one authorization model and their callers establish identity themselves), plus the admin pair `admin_enroll_participant` / `admin_remove_participation` (both refuse where a subscription is involved — enrolment onto a paid consumer club, removal that would strand a live sub). Three of these carry the audience gate and one deliberately does not — see §Audience.
- **Groups:** `apply_group_changes` (the sole write path for groups / gedu assignments / `participations.group_id`) + the read `get_product_groups_with_details` (groups + unassigned + waitlist snapshot).
- **Sessions (gedu):** the reads `get_gedu_group_feed` (one group's workspace in one round trip — stored rows, roster, schedule parameters; **no schedule expansion, the client owns the calendar math**) and `get_my_gedu_assignment_summaries` (one row per dashboard card, including the attention count, which is why the dashboard never fetches a feed); the writes `record_attendance` (one mark), `set_group_session_notes`, `set_group_notes`, `set_site_notes`, `set_group_member_minecraft`. All gedu-gated on the group assignment.
- **Sessions (family):** the read `get_my_family_product_feed` — one enrollment's whole page in one round trip, keyed by participation id and **self-scoping for both roles** (the participation's own participant — which admits a parent's own seat with no case of its own — or a parent linked to that gamer). It returns one JSONB document — the participant being read about, the product shell and schedule parameters, the group with its family-facing note, the site's public half, the gedus' first names, every stored session with its report, and that participant's marks — and deliberately nothing else, which is where the family privacy line is drawn. **No schedule expansion here either**, and one JSONB row is structurally immune to the PostgREST row cap a table select would meet. It refuses an unplaced participation, there being no group whose feed it could return.
- **Sessions / lifecycle, still unbuilt — check code:** `cancel_session`, `reschedule_session`, `request_substitute` / `assign_substitute`, `start_product`, `cancel_product`, `finalize_completed_products` do not exist.
- **Effective status:** `effective_status(product_id)` — SQL twin of the TS helper.

## Known gaps & traps

Things another agent would get wrong without this written down (the code doesn't announce them):

- **`update_product` nulls any editable column the form doesn't send.** It assigns *every* editable column on every call and each of its parameters defaults to NULL, so "not sent" and "clear this" are the same instruction. Any column on `products` that the admin form's payload doesn't carry therefore holds its seeded value only until someone edits that product, then silently becomes NULL — and because the form never showed the field, nobody sees it happen. Adding a column to the table is not enough; it has to reach the RPC too. Fix shape: the route re-reads the current value from the row and passes it back through, so an edit that doesn't touch a column can't clear it.

  The product picture no longer needs that fix, and the way it escaped is worth knowing because it is the stronger answer where it applies. The served path column is **derived, not written**: a product points at a catalogue entry by id, and a trigger on `products` sets the path from that entry on every write. No application code writes the path at all, so the RPC's own assignment of it is overwritten a moment later and cannot clear anything. Where a column has a single source of truth elsewhere in the database, deriving it in a trigger removes the whole class of problem rather than remembering to round-trip one more value.
  - **A column whose silent reset would be a policy change gets a *non-defaulted* parameter on both product RPCs, not a defaulted one.** The audience flags are the worked example: a default would let an omitting caller reset who a product is sold to without meaning to, whereas a non-defaulted parameter makes that caller fail loudly. It costs nothing to add at the moment a function is being dropped and recreated anyway — and a defaulted parameter would in any case create an overload PostgREST cannot disambiguate.
  - **The exception, and the shape to copy when the rule cannot apply: a column whose null is a *legal value* cannot use it.** The design tag is the worked example. A non-defaulted parameter is typed as required *and* non-nullable by codegen, so "clear this" would have no expressible wire shape at all — and unlike the ages beside the audience flags, no CHECK is standing by to catch an omission. The guard therefore moves up to the wire: **the body schema makes the field required-nullable on create and update alike**, so omission fails at the boundary, and an explicit null is mapped to an omitted argument whose default clears the column. Reach for this only when null is genuinely a value the column may hold; where null merely means "unset", the non-defaulted parameter above is still the right answer.
- **The signup CTA stays active when no `product_prices` row exists for the viewer's currency.** The form validates all currencies but the DB doesn't enforce it; gate the CTA on price availability.
- **Effective status and the registration CTA close at different moments for an event, and that is deliberate.** `effectiveStatus` is date-only, so an event goes `running` at local midnight on `start_date` and stays `running` until `end_date` passes — and the server-side `create_participation` gate (which accepts `pending` OR `running`) therefore takes signups through the whole of the event's day. The client CTA is the real gate: it keeps an event joinable — normal open/full/waitlist rules — until the event's **end instant** (`start_date` + its slot's `start_time` + `duration_minutes`, read in `products.timezone`), then flips to the disabled dead end. An event row with no schedule slot can't be timed and falls back to the midnight lock. Camps deliberately keep the midnight-of-`start_date` lock — a cohort starts together.
  - **Caveat: an event whose slot crosses local midnight closes at midnight, not at its end instant.** A 23:00 start with a 180-minute duration ends at 02:00 the following local day, by which point the date-only `end_date` comparison has already rolled the product to `completed` — so the CTA is `ended` (no button at all, not the "Already over" dead end) and the server-side gate has stopped accepting too. Both ends agree; the end-instant window is simply clipped at midnight. Nothing in the product schedules such events today.
- **The session cancellation/substitution ops, the lifecycle RPCs, and the grace-access gates are spec'd but unbuilt** — verify in code before relying on them. (Session *records* — attendance, reports, gedu notes — did ship; see §Sessions. Admin participation removal/enrolment *did* ship as RPCs; see §RPCs.)
- **Changing a product's audience does not clean up the people already enrolled under the old one.** An audience edit touches only the flags; it leaves existing `active` seats in place (a stale-but-visible enrollment, handled like an over-cap seat) **and** leaves any waitlist queue untouched — only turning the *waitlist* off (`waitlist_enabled = false`) ever purges a queue. That queue is exactly what lets `promote_from_waitlist` mint an audience-mismatched active seat (see §Audience — it is the fourth, deliberately ungated, path). The accepted answer is the same throughout: audience gates *entry*, never *rendering*, so a mismatched seat is a roster oddity an admin resolves by hand, not a broken state. If a future change ever needs the queue drained on an audience flip, that is a new decision, not a bug to backfill quietly.

## Deferred (design-now constraints)

- **On-platform municipality billing.** Today muni invoicing is fully offline (`external_contract`); a future `billing_mode='municipality_account'` brings it on-platform. Design for it now by branching on `billing_mode`, never on `product_type='municipality_club'`.
- **Purchased-state `/shop/[id]` layout** — today an enrolled viewer gets `AlreadySignedUpPanel`, and most of what this once wanted has landed elsewhere: the session record and the child's attendance live on the family product page, reached from My SOG rather than from the shop. What is left for `/shop/[id]` is the buying-side remainder — subscription management and adding another gamer — which is a question about whether a discovery surface should carry account actions at all.
- **A computed-tag mechanism is out of scope permanently, not deferred.** "New", "Popular", "Filling fast" and their kin have a different lifecycle and a different mechanism from an authored tag, and capacity information on browse cards is banned outright by an existing owner ruling (the municipality seat bar is the sole exception). Do not build one into the tag column.
- **Operational surfaces:** session cancellation / substitution UI, an admin view of session records, lifecycle buttons + reporting dashboards. (Attendance and reports shipped on both sides — the gedu writes them, the family reads them.)
